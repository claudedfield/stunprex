For a parent and a child of five to eight. Three colours sit around the child: one ahead, one to each side. The child picks a colour and says it, you roll the ball gently, and the child tries to send it that way with one touch. Any touch that goes roughly the right way is a success. Nothing is counted.

## Quick start

**You need:** a ball, 3 markers in different colours, and about 5 m by 5 m of flat ground.

**Set it out:**

```
              [colour 1]
                  |  about 1.5 m
   [colour 2] - (spot) - [colour 3]
                  |
                  |  about 4 m
               (you, with the ball)
```

- A small marker for the spot where the child receives (a cone, a chalk mark, a stone). The three colours sit about 1.5 m from it: one straight ahead (away from you), one to the left, one to the right. The child may choose which colour goes where. The sizes are suggestions.
- You stand about 4 m from the spot and roll the ball gently along the ground: no bounces, no hard passes.

**One rep:**

1. The child picks a colour and says it ("Red!"), or points to it.
2. You roll the ball gently to the spot.
3. One touch sends the ball towards that colour. A touch that ends near the marker, or clearly heads that way, is a success.
4. If it goes another way, roll again; nothing is said about the miss.
5. Ball back to you. The child picks again.

End on a rep that went the child's way.

**Load:**

- For 5 to 8: about 10 minutes, ending while they still want more; a drink and a rest whenever they like. Pain is never part of play.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a fall that hurts; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** look at your colour before the ball comes.

**Easier:** the child stands on the spot, the colours closer, the roll slower.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is play with the ball for a young child. The child chooses where the ball goes, and the first touch tries to send it there.

It is not a reaction game. Nobody calls the colour at the last moment; the choice is the child's, or, in the bib form, found by looking.

## How it grows

There are no levels. These are ways the game changes across sessions, as the child is ready.

- **First colours:** the child stands on the spot, picks a colour, and you roll. The child finds the colour and touches towards it.
- **Say it, then do it:** you wait with the ball after the child names the colour, so the choice comes first and the touch second.
- **A small walk in:** the child starts about 1.5 m behind the spot and walks forward as you roll. The first time receiving on the move.
- **A colour you can see:** before the roll, you lay one bib on the ground beside a colour marker. The rule of the game is that the ball goes to the colour with the bib, and the child finds it by looking. Move the bib between reps.
- **Two children:** a second child with their own spot and colours, side by side. Not a competition; each plays their own game.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- The body turning towards the colour before or as the ball arrives; even a half turn counts.
- A touch that heads the chosen way, however rough.
- The child looking at the colour before looking at the ball.
- The child naming a colour that is not the easiest one.

**During a rep:** stay quiet while the ball is rolling. Before the roll, a question may help ("which colour this time?"), never which way to touch.

**Praise** the intention: "You sent it that way. That's the game." "You looked at Blue before the ball came."

**Leave for later:** the hips, the body angle, which foot. The child uses whichever foot feels right; the open body comes from many tries, not from correction.

**If the child always picks the same colour,** ask with curiosity ("Has anyone been to Blue today?"), never as a correction.

**If the child stands square to you** and cannot reach the side colours, ask before the rep: "Can you turn a little so you can see Blue from the spot?"

**What not to do:** count the successes, compare with another day or another child, or call out the colour yourself.

**If interest drops,** stop, or change something: new colours, a new place for a colour, the bib.

## Why we think it helps

Sending the ball where you meant it to go is the start of a first touch with a purpose. Here the child chooses the direction before the ball arrives, so the touch carries the child's own intention, and the success line is generous so it comes often. Many gentle tries, with no score, let the body find the open position itself. If the child loves the game and never does it with friends, leave it: their games are theirs.

## Self-assessment

Asked lightly, after a few rolls, if the child is in the mood:

- "Which colour was the hardest to reach?"
- "Where did you look before the ball came?"
- "Which colour should we add next time?"
