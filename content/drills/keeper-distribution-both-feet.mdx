For a coach with three to six players. The keeper receives a back-pass while a forward closes in, and chooses where to play it: short through a gate on the side the forward has left free, or long past the press when both short sides are shut. Any player can take the keeper's role, and in a group without a specialist keeper every player takes it in turn.

## Quick start

**You need:** a goal, several balls, about 8 markers, and about 40 m by 30 m in front of the goal.

**Set it out:**

```
               [GOAL]
          +--------------+
          |      K       |
  [L] gate                 [R] gate      short gates, about
   about 15 m out, wide     15 m out, wide   3 m across
          |              |
          |  (F) forward starts about 14 m out
          |              |
             [LONG] gate, about 35 m out, central
          (S) server, about 20 m out
```

- Two short gates, each about 3 m across, about 15 m out and wide on either side.
- One long gate about 35 m out, centrally.
- The forward (F) starts about 14 m out, to one side. The server (S) plays the back-pass from about 20 m. All distances are suggestions.

**One rep:**

1. S plays the ball back to the keeper (K), along the ground, at a weight S varies.
2. As S plays, F closes K down from whichever side F chooses.
3. K takes a first touch and plays the ball through one of the three gates: short to the side F has left, or long when both short sides are shut.
4. The rep counts when the ball goes cleanly through a gate F could not block. A ball F intercepts or blocks is a turnover: F tries to score at once, and K defends the goal.
5. Roles move on after each block: K to server, S to forward, F to keeper or to the back of the line.

**With three:** K, S and F, as above. **With four to six:** players stand behind the short gates as outlets and receive the pass, or behind the long gate; they rotate in each block.

**The first block:** about 8 back-passes, then 2 to 3 minutes easy; two or three blocks in all. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the passing gets sloppier.

**Load:**

- Striking: about 8 passes a block, most of them short; long kicks in small numbers and only once warm; around a growth spurt, pain near a joint or at the front of the hip means stop, not push on.
- Contact: F closes to block the pass, never slides in and never makes contact with K. If K is about to be caught, the rep ends and K plays it again.
- F's closing is a short sprint: F walks back between reps, and the forward's role rotates every block.
- On a turnover the keeper goes down on a soft surface with the hands to the ball, and any knock to the head stops play for that player.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** know where F is and which gate is free before the ball arrives.

**Easier or harder:** easier, F walks in and always from the same side, so K can learn the open touch. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

The choice this drill trains is where the keeper plays the ball: which side, short or long. The task fixes the three gates and leaves the side, the length and the foot open; the forward's run decides what is free, and the keeper has to see it.

It does not train handling or saving. [Set and Secure](/training/keeper-set-position-handling) trains the keeper's set position and catch.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** F stands still on one side for a block. K plays through the gate on the other side, off either foot, and finds the touch that opens the body.
- **Level 2:** F walks in, from a side F chooses. K looks before the ball arrives and plays the free side.
- **Level 3:** F closes at full speed from a side F chooses, still without contact. K reads it and plays the free side, off the foot the side needs.
- **Level 4:** a second outfield player (D) may stand in front of one short gate or not, choosing each rep without saying. K judges whether to play short or long.
- **Level 5:** two forwards close, and the outlets behind the gates move to show for the ball. K finds a free player, not only a free gate, and a turnover is played out live.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The look before the ball.** Does K know where F is before the back-pass arrives, or only after the ball is at the feet?
- **The first touch.** Does it open the body away from F toward the free side, or leave K facing the own goal?
- **Short or long.** Does the choice change with what is free, or is it the same ball every time?
- **The foot.** When F closes from the side of K's stronger foot, does K play with the other foot, or turn back onto the stronger one?

**One question, between reps:** "Where was F when you took your first touch, and what did that leave free?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet while the ball is moving. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("where's the forward?"), never which side or which gate.

**Praise** the read and the clean pass, whatever happens after: "You looked before it came, opened away from F and played the free side. That was the choice."

**Leave for later:** the length and accuracy of the long ball. The look, the open touch and the short or long choice come first.

**When to simplify:** if K loses the ball to F most reps, go back to Level 1 or 2 until the open touch holds.

## Why we think it helps

A keeper with the ball at the feet starts the team's play, and the forward's run decides which side is open. This drill gives the keeper that read on every rep, with a forward who chooses the side, so the keeper learns to look before the ball comes and answer with the touch and the pass. We count the clean pass to the free gate, not the length of the kick. Playing out from both sides trains the keeper's weaker foot with deliberate practice, because a keeper who can use either foot has more options when the press comes. If a keeper plays out well here and not in games, put the back-pass into a small-sided game with a keeper and a press, and see whether the look before the ball is still there.

## Self-assessment

- "How did you know whether to play short or long today?"
- "Which foot did you avoid, and when did the forward make you use it?"
- "On your cleanest rep, what did your look and your first touch do?"
