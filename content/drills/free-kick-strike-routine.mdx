For one to four players, with a coach or on their own, and a keeper if there is one. Each player builds a short routine of their own before the kick, then chooses the strike (over the wall, around it, or low) and strikes it to the part of the goal the keeper leaves.

## Quick start

**You need:** a goal, a wall of mannequins or three poles or markers, several balls, and the area from the edge of the box to about 25 m out.

**Set it out:**

```
              [GOAL] + keeper
            +-----------------------+
            |  corners: the targets |
            |                       |
            |    [ wall, 9 m ]      |
            +-----------------------+
        (A) free-kick spot, about 18 to 25 m out
```

- The wall about 9 m from the ball, between the ball and one side of the goal. The wall is mannequins, poles or markers; players are not used as the wall in this drill.
- Three or four spots between about 18 and 25 m out, at different angles. All distances are suggestions.

**The routine (built first, the player's own):**

1. Place the ball and look at the target.
2. Choose the strike: over the wall, around it, or low past it.
3. The same steps back and the same angle each time, and one slow breath. Then strike the kick chosen.

The routine is the player's; the coach does not set its steps.

**One rep:**

1. A runs the routine and strikes.
2. The rep ends on a goal, a save, the wall or a miss. A fetches the ball; the next player goes.
3. After every few strikes, move the ball to a new spot.

Count strikes that beat the wall and reached the target, and whether the routine was the same, not only goals.

**With one:** no keeper; the corners are the target. **With two to four:** take turns, and a player who is not striking may keep goal with gloves.

**The first block:** 5 free kicks each, then 3 minutes easy (passing, walking, a drink); two or three blocks in all. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the contact gets sloppier.

**Load:**

- Striking: about 5 strikes a block, then the rest above; full-power strikes in small numbers and only once warm; around a growth spurt, pain near a joint means stop, not push on.
- The keeper goes down on a soft surface with the hands to the ball, and any knock to the head stops play for that player.
- With a keeper, swap the keeper every block.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** choose the kick before the run-up, then strike the kick you chose.

**Easier or harder:** easier, no wall and no keeper. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a technique drill with a choice in it: which free kick to strike, read from the wall and the keeper, and a routine that makes the kick the same each time.

It does not train finishing in open play. [One-Touch Finish](/training/first-time-finish-strike) trains a strike on a moving ball.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** no wall, no keeper. A finds a run-up and a contact that reach the target with the flight chosen.
- **Level 2:** A builds the routine and runs it on every kick.
- **Level 3:** add the wall. The strike has to clear it or go around it.
- **Level 4:** add the keeper. The keeper chooses where to stand each time; A reads it and picks the part of the goal left open.
- **Level 5:** a new spot every kick, the wall and keeper set, and one counted kick at the end of each block while the others watch. The routine and the strike are the score, as before. Nobody's counted kick is set against another player's.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The routine.** The same steps and the same breath each time, or rushed and different?
- **The choice and the commitment.** Does the player choose the kick before the run-up, or change it mid-run and scuff it?
- **The flight.** Does the plant foot and the contact match the kick chosen (over, around, low)?
- **The keeper.** At Level 4, does the target change with where the keeper stands?

**One question, between kicks:** "Which kick did you choose, and what told you?"

**During the run-up:** stay quiet. When a player is stuck, a question may help ("what's your routine?"), never which kick to strike.

**Praise** the routine and the clean strike, whatever the result: "Same steps, one breath, and you struck the kick you chose. The keeper got there; it was the right kick."

**Leave for later:** power and big bend. First a strike that repeats and reaches the target; more can be added once that holds.

**When to simplify:** if most kicks hit the wall, go back to Level 1 or move the spot further from the wall.

## Why we think it helps

A free kick is a still ball and time to think, and that time can unsettle a player as easily as it helps. This drill joins the strike to a short routine the player owns, so the kick starts the same way each time and the choice is made before the run-up. We judge a kick by the strike and the routine: a well-struck kick the keeper saves counts for more than a mishit that goes in. If a player strikes well here and rushes in games, ask them about the routine before anything else.

## Self-assessment

- "What is your routine, exactly, and did it stay the same on the counted kick?"
- "Which kick do you trust most: over, around or low?"
- "When did you change your mind during the run-up, and what happened to the strike?"
