For a coach with five to eight players aged thirteen and over. One small team keeps the ball; the other waits in a compact shape and presses only when the ball becomes winnable. Each pressing player reads that moment themselves, so the press goes together when it is on and holds when it is not.

## Quick start

**You need:** 4 corner cones, 2 mini-goals, bibs in two colours, 2 or 3 balls, and an area about 24 m by 20 m.

**Set it out:**

```
   [mini-goal]                 [mini-goal]
   +-----------------------------------+
   |                                   |
   |   team on the ball (3 or 4)       |
   |   pressing team (3 or 4)          |
   |   about 24 m x 20 m               |
   +-----------------------------------+
     end line: the pressing team scores
     by dribbling over it
```

- Two mini-goals at one end, about 14 m apart. The team on the ball scores by passing into either; the pressing team scores by winning the ball and dribbling it over the end line behind the team on the ball. No extra kit. The sizes are suggestions.
- Teams of 3 or 4; a spare player rotates in every 2 minutes. With five, 3 keep against 2 and swap; with seven, play Level 4's 4v3 from the start.
- The coach stands at the side with the spare balls.

**How it runs:**

1. The team on the ball keeps it and tries to pass into a mini-goal.
2. The pressing team holds a compact shape and waits. Each player reads for the moment the ball is winnable, and goes when they see it; the nearest teammates go with them to cut the easy passes. Nobody on the pressing team calls the press: each player reads it.
3. A ball won goes the other way, over the end line. A press on a ball that was not winnable usually leaves an easy way out, and the game shows it.
4. Play runs until a goal, the ball out, or about 40 seconds; then the coach plays a new ball in.
5. After the first game, the coach asks the pressing team when the ball was winnable. The players name the moments; the coach adds a missed moment as a question ("what about the ball played back?").

**The first block:** games of 2 minutes, then 2 minutes rest; four to six games, swapping roles each game. These numbers are a suggestion: shorten the games if the pressing gets slow.

**Load:**

- The press is a short, hard effort over and over. Rest between games is kept for every band; nobody plays on tired to make it harder. Stop the session when the pressing gets slower and sloppier, and never use extra running as a punishment.
- Tackles are for the ball, from the side or the front, never into the back. Split teams by size and maturity, not by age or level alone.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** wait, see it, go together.

**Easier or harder:** easier, the team on the ball plays one fewer. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a game problem for a small team. The choice it trains is each pressing player's: press now or hold, read from the ball and the player receiving it, and who goes to the ball and who closes a pass.

It does not train pressing after losing the ball. [The Five Seconds](/training/counter-press-five-seconds) trains that.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** the team on the ball plays at a slower pace (two touches each). The pressing team learns to wait and to see when the ball is winnable.
- **Level 2:** free touches. The moments come faster.
- **Level 3:** a pressing player who goes alone and is played round gives the team on the ball a point; a press where two or more go together and win it scores 2.
- **Level 4:** the team on the ball plays with one more player (4v3), so the press has to be sharper and more together to win it.
- **Level 5:** the area grows to about 30 m by 24 m, so the waiting shape has more ground to hold.

## A variation with a call

As a declared design, rated lower than the quiet game and never the daily default: one pressing player may call when they go ("going!"), so the others know the press has started. The call announces the caller's own action; every other player still reads whether the ball is winnable and where to go, and a press that only follows the call and does not cut a pass does not count as a clean press. Run the quiet game first.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The wait.** Does the pressing team stay compact and wait, or chase every pass?
- **The moment.** Does the press go when the ball is winnable (a heavy touch, a slow pass across or back, a receiver facing their own goal), or on a ball under control?
- **Together.** When one player goes, do the next two go with them to close the passes, or does one chase alone?

**One question, between games:** "When did you go, what did you see, and who went with you?"

**During play:** stay quiet. Questions wait for the break.

**Praise** the read and the wait: "You held three passes and went on the heavy touch, and two of you went together."

**Leave for later:** the exact angles of the press. First the wait and going together.

**When to simplify:** if the pressing team chases every pass, go back to Level 1.

## Why we think it helps

A press tends to fail in one of two ways: someone goes when the ball is not winnable and leaves a hole, or nobody goes when it is. Both are a read, not effort. This game makes those moments come again and again in a small space, and asks every pressing player to see them, not to wait for a shout. If a team presses well here and not in matches, move the same scoring into a bigger game.

## Self-assessment

- "What told you the ball was winnable?"
- "When did you go too early, and what did it leave open?"
- "Did you ever wait for someone else to go? What did you see yourself?"
