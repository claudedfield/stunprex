For a player alone, or a coach with a few players. The ball in a match is often not the one you trained with: wet and heavy, a futsal ball in a hall, a different size. This circuit puts three different balls in a row. The player dribbles a short zone with each, and at each swap finds, on the first touches, how the new ball wants to be touched.

## Quick start

**You need:** three different balls (a standard ball, a futsal ball and a heavier ball: a wet ball, or one a size up, dribbled only, never struck hard or headed), 8 to 10 cones, and about 24 m by 2 m of ground.

**Set it out:**

```
  START                                                  END
    |  Zone A, about 8 m  | S |  Zone B, about 8 m  | S |  Zone C, about 8 m  |
    |  standard ball      |   |  futsal ball        |   |  heavier ball       |

  S = swap cone, with the next ball waiting at it
```

- Three zones of about 8 m in a line. A small cone at the start and end of each zone; a bigger cone (or a bib over a cone) at each swap, so it stands out while you dribble.
- The futsal ball waits at the first swap cone, the heavier ball at the second. Tape on the balls (none, one stripe, two stripes) lets the player see from a distance which ball is coming.
- For 5 to 8: two balls only (a standard ball and a futsal ball, or a smaller one), zones of about 5 m. No weighted ball for 5 to 8.
- All the sizes and times are suggestions.

**One run:**

1. Dribble Zone A with the standard ball at a pace you control, not a sprint.
2. At the swap cone, leave the ball and take the futsal ball in one movement. Dribble Zone B.
3. At the next swap, leave it and take the heavier ball. Dribble Zone C.
4. At the end, leave the ball and walk back, putting each ball back at its place.

The first touches on a new ball will often run long or stop short. That is the drill: the player notices and changes the next touch. Nobody corrects it.

**Load:**

- About 30 to 45 seconds between runs (the walk back). A block is 6 to 8 runs, about 5 to 7 minutes; about 90 seconds and a drink between blocks. For 5 to 8: one or two blocks, ending while they still want more. For 9 to 12: two blocks, no level built on being tired. From 13: two or three blocks.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a fall that hurts; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** what did the first touch on the new ball tell you?

**Easier or harder:** easier, two balls and shorter zones, at walking pace. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

The choice this drill trains is the weight of the next touch, made from what the last touch on a new ball told the player. The task fixes the balls and the zones; the touch, the foot and the pace are the player's.

It is not a race. A quick circuit with the ball running away is not better than a slower one with the ball close. It is a supporting drill: there is no opponent and no teammate, and it trains the touch, not a game decision.

## Levels

For 5 to 8, every level keeps the two balls.

- **Level 1:** two balls (standard and futsal), zones of about 5 m, slow. Can be run out and back, swapping again on the way back.
- **Level 2:** the three balls, zones of about 8 m, as in the quick start. The player uses whichever foot they like.
- **Level 3:** each zone in two halves: the first half with one foot, the second with the other. The player keeps track of the halfway point without a marker.
- **Level 4:** spare balls of each kind lie at a few places along the circuit. A helper beside the circuit raises a bib now and then, without a word; when the player sees it, they leave the ball they have and take the nearest spare, wherever they are. The swap comes without warning, so the player cannot get ready for it.
- **Level 5:** where you have different ground (grass, a hard surface, a hall and outside), put the zone boundaries on the changes of ground, so a new ball and new ground come together. Without it: the three balls at a quicker pace the player chooses, with one surprise swap per run.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- The second touch after a swap. A first touch that runs long and a second that is shorter shows the player noticing. The same touch in every zone shows they are not yet noticing.
- A look at the new ball as the player picks it up.
- The pace: does the player keep it, or does the futsal ball pull them faster and the heavier ball slow them?
- The approach to the swap cone: arriving with the ball close to the foot.

**During a run:** stay quiet. Between runs, a question may help: "how did that ball feel different from the first one?", "how heavy did your next touch need to be?"

**Praise** the noticing: "Your first touch ran long and you shortened the second." "You looked at the ball before you touched it."

**Leave for later:** the speed of the circuit, how the player dribbles within a zone, the other foot before Level 3, and a tidy swap in the first sessions.

**If the player touches every ball the same way:** slow the pace until they can feel the difference, then ask what it was.

**If the player is frustrated after a few runaway touches on one ball:** ask how it went last week on that ball, and compare only with that.

**What not to do:** time the player against anyone, count runaway touches for them, or tell them how hard to touch the ball.

## Why we think it helps

The ball changes from match to match and from pitch to pitch. A player who has met many balls, and who has to find the touch on each one in the first few contacts, has more to draw on than one who only knows one ball. We train the weaker foot with deliberate practice, because a player who can use either foot has more options. If the player does well here and still struggles with a new ball in a game, take this circuit to new places: a wet day, a hall, a park.

## Self-assessment

After a session, the player may note how many first touches on a new ball ran away in a block, and compare it only with their own note from last week. Then:

- "Which ball took you longest to get used to?"
- "What did you change in your touch for it?"
- "Where else could you try a different ball this week?"
