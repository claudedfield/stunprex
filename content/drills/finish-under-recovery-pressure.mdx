For a coach with two to four players and, ideally, a keeper. One player receives a pass with a defender recovering from behind, and has to set the ball with the first touch and choose a finish before the defender gets there.

## Quick start

**You need:** a goal with a keeper (or a mini-goal), 6 markers, several balls, and about 20 m of pitch in front of the goal.

**Set it out:**

```
              [GOAL] + keeper
        +--------------------------+
        |      finishing area      |
        |                          |
        |   (A) marker, about 16 m |
        |   (D) marker, 6 m behind |
        |                          |
   (S) server, to one side and behind
```

- One marker about 16 m from goal for A, and one about 6 m behind it for D. Two more for the server, one to each side, about 10 m back. Two to mark the edges of the finishing area if you have no box. All distances are suggestions.

**One rep:**

1. The server (S, a player or the coach) passes to A, from either side, without saying which.
2. D may go the moment the pass is played, and recovers toward A to close the shot.
3. A takes a first touch into a place to finish from, away from D, then finishes before D arrives.
4. The rep ends on a goal, a save, a miss, or D getting a foot to the ball.
5. Rotate every two reps: A to D, D to S, S to A. The keeper swaps in after each block.

**With two:** the coach serves and the two players swap between A and D. **With four:** the fourth keeps goal or waits at the server's side.

**The first block:** 4 finishes each, then 3 minutes easy (passing, walking, a drink); two or three blocks in all. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the runs or the strikes get slower.

**Load:**

- D's recovery is a short sprint. The rotation and the walk back come before the next one. Stop when the runs get slower and sloppier, and never use extra reps as a punishment.
- About 4 full strikes a block, as above, and only once warm. Around a growth spurt, pain near a joint means stop, not push on.
- Pair players by size and maturity, not by age or level alone. D plays for the ball and may block the shot, never into the back or across the legs. The keeper goes down on a soft surface with the hands to the ball, and any knock to the head stops play for that player.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** where does the first touch need to go so the finish is open and the chaser is behind you?

**Easier or harder:** easier, D starts further back. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a game problem with one attacker, one chaser and a keeper. The choice the player makes is the first touch and the finish, read from where the chaser is and what the keeper does.

It does not train the run that gets the player into the space, or a long breakaway. [The Breakaway](/training/breakaway-finish-fatigue) trains the long carry to a finish with a chaser.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** D starts about 6 m behind. The window is wide: a touch, a look, a finish.
- **Level 2:** D starts about 4 m behind. The window is shorter.
- **Level 3:** the finish is with the other foot. The first touch has to set it up.
- **Level 4:** the server mixes passes along the ground and in the air. A reads the ball early and sets the touch to it.
- **Level 5:** the keeper is fully live and may come out, D starts level, and A has one touch to set and one to finish.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The first touch.** Does it take the ball away from D and into a place to finish from, or toward D?
- **The finish.** Chosen and placed, or a rushed blast?
- **Giving up.** When D is close, does A still finish, or stop as if the chance is gone?

**One question, between reps:** "Where did your first touch take you, and what did that leave?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("where's the space?"), never where to shoot.

**Praise** the touch and the choice, whatever the result: "Your touch took you away from them and you picked the corner. The keeper made a good save; that was a good rep."

**Leave for later:** the striking technique, if the player is rushing. First the touch and the calm; the contact sharpens once the player stops hurrying.

**When to simplify:** if most finishes are rushed, go back to Level 1 and move D further back.

## Why we think it helps

In a game a chance often comes with a defender closing and little time. This drill puts that pressure in from the first rep, with a window the player can make if the touch is good and the choice is quick. We judge a rep by the touch and the finish: a well-placed shot the keeper saves counts for more than a scuffed one that goes in. A rushed miss is worth one question about what broke (the touch, the look, or the strike). If the player finishes well here and still rushes in games, put the same picture into a small-sided game with a keeper and a long pitch.

## Self-assessment

- "When you finished well, what did your first touch do for you?"
- "Did the chaser change your finish? Could you stay calm and place it?"
- "Which finishes felt rushed, and what would you change next time?"
