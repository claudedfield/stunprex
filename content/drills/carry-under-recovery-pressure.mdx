For a coach with two to four players. One player carries the ball down a channel while a defender recovers from behind or alongside, and has to keep the ball on the far side of the body and choose when to drive on and when to let it go.

## Quick start

**You need:** 6 markers (or 4 markers and a mini-goal), several balls, and a strip of pitch about 25 m long.

**Set it out:**

```
   START                                          TARGET
     +-------------------------------------------- [T]  gate 2 m wide
     |  channel about 25 m x 12 m                 |
     +--------------------------------------------
   (A) starts with the ball;  (D) starts level or just behind
```

- Four markers for the corners of a channel about 25 m long and 12 m wide, and two for a target gate about 2 m wide at the far end (or a mini-goal). All three sizes are suggestions.
- D starts level with A or a step behind, on one side. D chooses the side and the distance each rep.

**One rep:**

1. A goes when ready. D may go the moment A touches the ball.
2. A drives toward the target. D recovers and tries to get alongside and win the ball.
3. A protects the ball as D arrives: body between D and the ball, touches onto the far foot, the line shaded away from D's reach.
4. The rep ends when A dribbles through the target under control, D wins the ball, or the ball leaves the channel.
5. A and D walk back along the outside and swap roles.

**With three:** the third player stands near the target as a release option from Level 4, and rotates in after each block. **With four:** two pairs take turns, so each pair rests while the other runs.

**The first block:** 4 carries each, then 3 minutes easy (passing, walking, a drink); two or three blocks in all. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the runs get slower.

**Load:**

- Each carry is a short burst at full effort. The walk back and the other pair's turn come before the next one. Stop when the runs get slower and sloppier, and never use extra runs as a punishment.
- Pair players by size and maturity, not by age or level alone. D plays for the ball, shoulder to shoulder at most, never into the back or across the legs. Any knock to the head stops play for that player.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** which foot is the ball on when the defender arrives?

**Easier or harder:** easier, D only shadows and reaches lightly. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a game problem with one carrier and one recovering defender. The choice the player makes is the line, the foot, and when to drive on or release, read from where the defender is coming from.

It does not train beating a defender who stands in front of you, or finishing. [Slow to Fast](/training/change-of-pace-1v1) trains a carry against a defender in front.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** D shadows at a jog and reaches lightly. A finds the shape: body between, ball on the far foot, at a pace A can control.
- **Level 2:** D recovers at full effort and tries to win the ball.
- **Level 3:** D starts from a different place each rep (behind, from the side, cutting across). A reads the angle and changes the line and the foot.
- **Level 4:** a teammate near the target offers for a pass. A chooses drive on or release as D closes; a clean release scores the same as a carry through.
- **Level 5:** two defenders recover from different sides and the channel ends in a goal. A protects, decides and finishes or releases. The contact line above holds for both defenders.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **Body between defender and ball.** Is the ball on the far foot as D arrives, or out on the near side where D can reach it?
- **The line.** Does A bend the run away from D's reach, or run straight and let D get alongside on the ball side?
- **Drive or release.** Does the choice change with where D is, or is it the same every time?
- **Still going forward.** Does A keep moving while shielding, or slow to a stop and get crowded out?

**One question, between reps:** "Where was the defender when you chose, and which foot was the ball on?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("which foot?"), never whether to drive on or release.

**Praise** the shape and the choice, whatever the result: "You kept your body in the way and let it go just before they got there. That was a good read."

**Leave for later:** the quality of the release pass. First the body shape and the far-foot touch; the timing of the release sharpens once the ball stays protected.

**When to simplify:** if D wins most balls on the first contact, go back to Level 1 and widen the channel.

## Why we think it helps

In a game, a carry often gets a defender alongside before it gets anywhere. This drill gives the carrier that defender from a new angle each time, so the player builds a way of protecting the ball that changes with the angle, not one rehearsed move. We judge a rep by the shape and the choice: a clean release just before the tackle counts as much as a carry through. A lost ball is worth one question about what broke (the body, the foot, or the choice). If the player protects the ball well here and still loses it in games, put the same picture into a small-sided game where carries are allowed and tackles from the side are common.

## Self-assessment

- "How did you keep the ball away from the defender? What worked?"
- "When should you have let it go earlier instead of driving on?"
- "Which angle was hardest to protect against, and why?"
