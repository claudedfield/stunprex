For a coach with six to ten players aged thirteen and over. Two small teams play to four small goals; when a team loses the ball, it has five seconds to win it back together, or it drops back together. The players decide which.

## Quick start

**You need:** 4 mini-goals (or 8 markers as small goals), 4 markers for the corners, bibs in two colours, several balls beside the pitch, and an area about 30 m by 24 m.

**Set it out:**

```
   [goal]                 [goal]
   +-----------------------+
   |                       |
   |   two teams, 3v3      |
   |   or 4v4              |
   |   about 30 m x 24 m   |
   +-----------------------+
   [goal]                 [goal]
```

- Two small goals at each end, near the corners. Each team attacks two and defends two.
- Two teams of 3 or 4. With 7 or 9 players, one rests and swaps in after each game; with 10, two rest.
- The coach stands at the side with the spare balls.

**How it runs:**

1. The teams play. When a team loses the ball, it has five seconds to win it back.
2. The coach counts the five seconds silently and says nothing during play. The players read from the game whether the press is beaten.
3. A ball won back inside five seconds and then scored scores 2. Any other goal scores 1. The coach gives the score when the goal goes in ("two" or "one").
4. If the players see the press is beaten, they drop back together towards their own goals instead of chasing. That choice is theirs, and a good drop back is praised as much as a ball won.
5. When the ball goes out or a goal is scored, the coach plays a new ball in at once, to either team, without warning.

**The first block:** games of 2 minutes, then 2 minutes rest; four to six games in all. These numbers are a suggestion: shorten the games if the pressing gets slow.

**Load:**

- The press is a short, hard effort over and over. Rest between games is real rest. Stop the session when the pressing gets slower and sloppier, and never use extra running as a punishment.
- Tackles are for the ball, from the side or the front, never into the back. Pair or split teams by size and maturity, not by age or level alone.
- Rest between games is kept for every band; nobody plays on tired to make it harder.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** the moment you lose it, go, together, or drop back, together.

**Easier or harder:** easier, play 3v3 on a slightly bigger area. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a game problem for a small team. The choice it trains is the team's: press now, or drop back, and the player's own: who goes to the ball and who closes a pass.

It does not train the shape a team drops back into or a full-pitch pressing plan; those need a bigger game.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** a team that wins the ball must make one pass before it can score. This gives the pressing team a moment to react and learn the window.
- **Level 2:** the one-pass rule goes. Players have to react straight away.
- **Level 3:** a good drop back now scores too: if the team that won the ball cannot get a shot away within ten seconds because the other team got back into place, the defending team gets 1 point. The coach gives that point after the attack ends, never during it.
- **Level 4:** when the coach plays a new ball in, it sometimes goes to a team with one more player for that phase (a resting player joins for one attack). The press has to be sharper against more players. With no one resting, the coach joins the team for one attack, or skip Level 4.
- **Level 5:** the coach plays new balls in faster, so turnovers come more often. Rest between games is kept; Level 5 makes play denser through more turnovers, never through longer games or shorter rest.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The speed of the switch.** Does the player who lost the ball react straight away, or take a moment first?
- **Pressing together.** Does the nearest player go while teammates close the easy passes, or does one player chase alone?
- **The drop back.** When the press is beaten, does the team get back together, or keep chasing and leave the goals open?

**One question, between games:** "When did you press, when did you drop back, and what told you which?"

**During play:** nothing but the score when a goal goes in. Questions and cues wait for the break.

**Praise** the quick reaction, especially after a frustrating loss: "You lost it and won it back two seconds later. No pause." Praise a good drop back the same way.

**Leave for later:** exact pressing angles. First the quick reaction and going together; who closes which pass sharpens once that is there.

**When to simplify:** if most turnovers bring no reaction at all, go back to Level 1.

## Why we think it helps

Just after a team wins the ball it is often spread out and not yet ready. A team that reacts in those seconds can win the ball back close to goal. Losing the ball stings, and for a moment a player can switch off. This game gives a player many small losses in a short time, and a reason to react at once, so reacting becomes the habit. If the team presses well here and not in matches, the next step is a bigger game with the same scoring, not more of this one.

## Self-assessment

- "How quickly did you switch from attacking to winning it back? What slowed you down when you were slow?"
- "When did pressing work, and when should you have dropped back?"
- "What do you do with the frustration of losing the ball, so it does not slow your reaction?"
