For a coach with one to three keepers and a server. The keeper sets the feet as the ball is about to be played, reads where it is coming, gets the body behind it and holds it. The server varies the height and the side without saying which, so every ball is a small read. Any player can take the keeper's role; the server's role is a player's job too, and it trains the weight and the disguise of the feed.

## Quick start

**You need:** a goal (a small one is fine), several balls, 4 markers, and about 15 m in front of the goal.

**Set it out:**

```
        [GOAL]
   +--------------+
   |  K sets here |   two markers mark the keeper's
   +--------------+   working width on the goal line
         |
         |  about 8 to 10 m
         |
        (S) server, between two markers
```

- Two markers on the goal line mark the keeper's working width; two more mark where the server stands, about 8 to 10 m out. All distances are suggestions.

**One rep:**

1. K starts relaxed in the middle of the goal.
2. As S is about to play, K sets: feet about shoulder-width, knees soft, weight forward, hands ready in front.
3. S throws (or, at the higher levels, strikes) the ball at a height and to a side S chooses, without saying which.
4. K moves the body behind the ball where it can, catches it and brings it into the body.
5. The rep counts on a ball caught and held. A spill is reset at once: K sets again for the next ball, no dwelling.
6. Roles move on after each block: K to server, S to keeper or to the back of the line.

**With one keeper:** the coach or a parent serves. **With two or three:** they take turns in goal and as server.

**The first block:** about 10 balls, then 1 to 2 minutes easy; two or three blocks in all. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the catching gets sloppier.

**Load:**

- Young bands (9 to 12): thrown balls through Level 2; struck balls at a pace the keeper catches cleanly, never full power at a young keeper; shorter blocks and more rest.
- Striking, for the server at Levels 3 and 5: about 10 balls a block, at a catchable pace; around a growth spurt, pain near a joint means stop, not push on.
- Contact with the ground: there is no diving on this page. A ball the keeper cannot reach standing or with a step is let go.
- Any ball to the face or any knock to the head stops play for that keeper.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** be set just as the ball is played, not before and not after.

**Easier or harder:** easier, S throws to the chest from the same spot each time. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a supporting technique drill with a small read in it: the set, the catch and the hold, with the height and the side read from the server and the flight. The server chooses, the keeper reads. It sits beside a keeper's game drill, not in place of one.

It does not train distribution, crosses or one against one. [Play It Out](/training/keeper-distribution-both-feet) trains the keeper with the ball at the feet, and [Come or Stay](/training/keeper-1v1-come-or-stay) the keeper facing a player through on goal.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** S throws at catchable heights, chest and waist, from the middle. K finds the set and the catch.
- **Level 2:** S varies the height (chest, waist, along the ground) and the side, without saying which. K reads the throw and changes the body and hands for it.
- **Level 3:** S strikes the ball from the hands or the ground at a catchable pace, instead of throwing it. K reads a faster flight and still sets and holds.
- **Level 4:** K starts a step off-centre and has to move to get behind the ball before the catch.
- **Level 5:** two servers, standing apart, play one after the other at a pace each chooses. K catches, sets again quickly and faces the next ball, with the reset after a spill part of the task.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The set.** Feet planted and weight forward as the ball is played, or flat-footed, or still moving?
- **The body.** Behind the ball where it can be, or reaching with the hands alone?
- **The hold.** Caught and brought into the body, or pushed away so a rebound follows?
- **After a spill.** Does K set again for the next ball, or stay with the last one?

**One question, between reps:** "Where were your feet when the ball was played?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet while the ball is moving. When a keeper is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("when did you set?"), never where the ball is going.

**Praise** the set and the hold: "Set as it was played, body behind it, and you held it in."

**Leave for later:** diving and spectacular saves. The set, the catch and the hold come first, with their own pages and load lines when the time comes.

**When to simplify:** if most balls are spilled, go back to Level 1 and throw slower until the catch holds.

## Why we think it helps

A keeper who is set as the ball is played, and gets the body behind it, has the best chance of holding it, and a held ball gives the opponent no second chance. This drill gives a keeper many catches in a short time, each a little different because the server chooses, so the keeper reads every ball rather than repeat one catch. The reset after a spill is practised in the same block, so a keeper gets used to letting one ball go and meeting the next. If a keeper catches well here and not in games, put the catch into a small-sided game with shots from different angles and see whether the set is still there.

## Self-assessment

- "When did your set feel best, and what were your feet doing?"
- "Which catches did you hold, and which got away from you?"
- "What do you do after a spill, so the next ball is caught?"
