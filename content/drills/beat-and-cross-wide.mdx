For a coach with two to five players and, ideally, a keeper. One player beats a defender in the wide channel and then has to choose the cross from what is in the box and where the covering defender is. Every player takes the wide role, the defending roles and the runs in turn.

## Quick start

**You need:** a goal with a keeper, or the three zones alone, 7 markers, several balls, and one side of the pitch from about 30 m out to the goal line.

**Set it out:**

```
   goal line  [GOAL] + keeper
        zones: near post (1), far post (2), cut-back spot (3)
        +----------------------------------+------------+
        |   box          (B) cover         |   (D)      |
        |                                  |  channel   |
        |                                  |  10 m wide |
        |                                  |   (A)      |
        +----------------------------------+------------+
   channel about 10 m wide and 30 m long, along the touchline
```

- Four markers for a channel about 10 m wide and 30 m long along the touchline, ending at the goal line. A starts at the far end with the ball, D about 10 m in front.
- Three markers in the box for the target zones: near post, far post, and the cut-back spot about 10 m out. Without a goal, the zones are the target. All sizes are suggestions.

**One rep:**

1. A goes when ready and takes on D, down the line or inside.
2. The cover (B) starts on the edge of the box and may go the moment A touches the ball, to block the cross.
3. Once past, A reads the box and B, and crosses to the zone that is open: early to the near or far post, or a cut-back from near the goal line.
4. The rep ends on a cross into a zone or a goal, a block, the ball going out, or D winning it.
5. Everyone walks back and moves one role on: A to the runner in the box (from Level 5), the runner to B, B to D, D to A.

**With two:** A and D, no cover, crosses to the zones. **With three to five:** add B, then runners in the box at Level 5. **Both sides:** every second block runs from the other touchline, so each player crosses from both sides.

**The first block:** 4 attempts each in the wide role, then 3 minutes easy (passing, walking, a drink); two or three blocks in all. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the runs or the crosses get slower.

**Load:**

- Each rep is a short burst at full effort. The walk back and the rotation come before the next one. Stop when the runs get slower and sloppier, and never use extra reps as a punishment.
- Crosses are full strikes: about 4 a block, as above, and only once warm. Around a growth spurt, pain near a joint means stop, not push on.
- Pair players by size and maturity, not by age or level alone. Defenders play for the ball, never into the back or across the legs. The keeper goes down on a soft surface with the hands to the ball, and any knock to the head stops play for that player.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** once you are past, where is the cover, and which zone does that leave open?

**Easier or harder:** easier, D only shadows and there is no cover. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a game problem in the wide channel. The choice the player makes is how to get past and which cross to play, read from the defender, the cover and the box.

It does not train the run into the box or the finish. [Beat and Decide](/training/beat-and-decide-1v1) trains the choice after a beat in the middle of the pitch.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** D shadows and does not tackle, no cover. Beat D, cross to any zone.
- **Level 2:** D defends at full effort.
- **Level 3:** add the cover. A crosses to the zone the cover leaves open.
- **Level 4:** from both sides in the same block, so the cross comes off either foot. Count the crosses that reached a zone, not which foot played them.
- **Level 5:** one or two runners attack the box, D and the cover are fully live. Runners make their runs without calling; A reads the runs and the cover, and crosses to a player. The contact line above holds for everyone.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The beat makes room.** Does A get past into space to cross from, or cross from where D can block?
- **The cross before the block.** Once past, does A cross in time, or take a touch more and let the cover arrive?
- **The choice.** Does the zone change with where the cover is, or is it the same every time?
- **The ball.** Does A run the ball out of play after the beat, or keep it close enough to cross?

**One question, between reps:** "Once you were past, where was the cover, and what did that leave?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("where's the cover?"), never which cross to play.

**Praise** the joined sequence, whatever the result: "You got past and crossed before they could block it. That was the right moment, even with the keeper taking it."

**Leave for later:** the polish of the beating move and of the cross on its own. First the link between the two; each half sharpens once the link holds.

**When to simplify:** if most crosses are blocked, go back to Level 2 and take the cover out until the cross comes in time.

## Why we think it helps

In a game, getting past a defender out wide is worth something when a cross follows before the cover arrives. This drill joins the two, so the player looks for the cross as they beat the defender and learns how much time they have. We judge a rep by the joined sequence: a timely cross to the open zone counts, even if nobody scores from it. A broken rep is worth one question about what broke (the beat, the time taken, or the choice of cross). Because every player takes every role, it is also a way to try the wide role, not a way to settle who plays there.

## Self-assessment

- "When you got past the defender, did you already have a cross in mind? Did it help?"
- "On your best reps, what let you cross in time?"
- "Which side and which foot do you want more reps on next time?"
