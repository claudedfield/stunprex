## Quick start

**You need:** a ball, 20 markers, 8 cards, a pen and paper, a chair or a box, and a phone timer, which sits on the chair. Cones, shoes and bottles all work as markers. You need flat ground about 10 m × 10 m. You run it alone. Setting it out takes about five minutes the first time.

**Set it out:**

```
            [chair with the cards]
      •─────────────────────────────•
      │     •        •        •     │
      •        •         •          •
 gate L                              gate R
      •    •        •        •      •
      │        •              •     │
      │    •        •               │
      •─────────────────────────────•
              [you come in here]
```

- **The square:** 8 m × 8 m, a marker on each corner (4 markers).
- **The field:** 12 markers scattered inside, with no straight path through them.
- **Two gates:** each is two markers 1 m apart, one in the middle of the left side (gate L) and one in the middle of the right side (gate R), as you see them from where you come in (4 markers).
- **The cards:** write L on four cards and R on four. Shuffle them without looking and put them face down on a chair or box that touches the far side of the square, in the middle. Put something small on top if it is windy.

**One round lasts 3 minutes.** Start the timer and dribble in from the near side.

1. **Dribble among the markers.** Change feet with every touch. Count slowly to fifteen in your head.
2. **Go to the cards.** Keep the ball moving with small touches, turn the top card over with your hand, read it, and put it down to the right of the pile.
3. **Leave by the gate it names,** by any route you like. The ball goes through first, then you. On the way to the gate you may use either foot.
4. **Come back in** by the same gate and start again at step 1.

**The round ends** when the timer rings or the cards run out. If you are on your way to a gate, finish that one.

**The count:** every time the ball touches a marker in the field, say the running number out loud ("one", "two") and carry on. Nothing is lost. It is a number to write down.

**When it goes wrong:**

- The ball leaves the square anywhere but the gate: bring it back in where it went out and carry on.
- The ball touches a gate marker on the way out: go through anyway. That exit is not a clean one. On your next visit to the chair, before you turn the next card, move that card across to the left of the pile. If it was your last exit, move it in the rest.
- You have to stop the ball to turn the card: do it, and try to keep it moving at the next card.
- Two cards come up together: use the top one and put the other back.

**The first block:** one round at an easy pace, about a slow jog, then 90 seconds rest. Stop if something hurts, or if you are tired and the touches are getting worse.

**One thing to think about:** keep the ball moving at the chair.

**Easier or harder:** easier is 8 markers in the field. Harder is 15, which takes three more markers.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a supporting drill for practising on your own: carrying the ball through a crowded space to an exit you did not know a moment ago. The markers are what crowd the space; they are the equipment, not the drill.

**The choice it trains** is your route through the markers to a gate you did not know a moment ago. The square, the gates and the card are fixed. The route and the touches are yours.

The card does one job: it stops you from knowing the gate in advance. It is not something you would look for in a game, and reading it is not the same as looking around a pitch. The markers do not move and nobody tries to take the ball. So this drill works on close control with both feet while part of your attention is somewhere else. It does not work on reading opponents or teammates.

## A full session

- Three rounds of 3 minutes, with 90 seconds rest after each. With setting out, about 20 minutes.
- In each rest, write down two numbers: the marker touches you counted, and the clean exits out of the cards you turned. A clean exit is a ball through the gate without touching either gate marker. The cards on the right of the pile are your clean exits, and the ones on the left are the others. For example: 7 touches, 4 clean exits of 6.
- Then move four or five of the field markers to new places and shuffle the cards again.
- Read your numbers across sessions, not inside one. Compare them only with your own earlier numbers. They say nothing about anybody else.
- **When you have finished,** ask yourself where in the square most of the touches happened, and what you would do differently there.
- **On less space,** a square of 6 m × 6 m with 8 markers in the field works (16 markers in all).

## What good looks like

- The ball stays within a step of you among the markers.
- Both feet are working in the field.
- At the chair, the ball is still rolling while your hand turns the card.
- You pick your way to the gate from a distance and arrive at it straight on.
- You count every touch, including the ones nobody would have seen.

## Ways to change it

Stay with the base version until most of your exits in a session are clean. Then try one of these. You do not have to take them in order.

- **Foot cards (harder).** Write a foot under each letter: "L, left foot", "L, right foot", "R, left foot", "R, right foot", two cards of each. The last touch through the gate is made with that foot. Go back to plain cards if you keep forgetting the foot.
- **A third gate (harder).** Add gate N in the middle of the near side, where you came in, with two more markers. Write a new set of eight cards for this version: three L, three R, two N. Go back to two gates if you find yourself standing still to work out where to go.
- **Count down (harder).** After you read the card, count down out loud from eight and be through the gate before zero. In this version keep the marker count in your head and say only the countdown. Drop the countdown if the marker touches go up sharply.
- **Two cards (harder).** Turn two cards over, one after the other, and leave by both gates in that order before you go back to the chair. If both say the same gate, go out, come in, and go out again. Go back to one card if you forget the second.
- **A helper calls (different, not harder).** Somebody stands outside the square and calls "L" or "R" in place of the cards, at moments of their own choosing. This is the Cone Sea with a coach. The helper calls the gate and nothing else.

## For a parent or coach

The drill runs without you. Help with the setting out the first time, and check that the chair touches the far side.

**Watch for:**

- the ball stopped dead at the chair on every visit;
- the same route to a gate every time, even after the markers have moved;
- a count that is lower than what you saw. Say that it is a measurement and nothing more. If it says nine, nine is where today starts.

**Questions to ask:**

- "Which foot did most of the work in that round?"
- "Where in the square did most of the touches happen?"
- "When did you know which way you would go to the gate?"

**If you praise,** praise a ball kept moving at the chair or an honest count, not a fast round.

**Leave alone for now:** messy first tries at the chair, and a slow pace. Turning a card with a ball at your feet is new, and the first round or two may look clumsy.

**Make it easier, or stop, when:**

- the rules confuse the player: do one round with the cards face up, so the order is known, then shuffle;
- the ball hits markers all the time and nothing changes: take four markers out of the field;
- the player has had enough: finish for today;
- something hurts: stop.

## Why we think it helps

This is our reasoning, and we have not tested it. A field of markers gives a lot of small changes of direction in a short time, and the rule about feet gives half of them to the weaker foot. The card means you cannot plan the way out before you start, so the route has to be found with the ball already at your feet.

**Where it should show later:** in a small game or a crowded playground game, you keep the ball close with either foot in a tight space, and you can change where you are going without losing it.

**What would count against it:** your rounds here get clean, and in a tight space in a game the ball still gets away from you, or you still use one foot only.
