For a coach with three or four players aged thirteen and over. A receiver takes the ball with a defender on their back and has to choose, before or on the first touch, whether to turn away and out through a gate, or to protect the ball and set it back to the server.

## Quick start

**You need:** 4 cones as two gates (or 2 mini-goals) and a ball.

**Set it out:**

```
        [gate L]             [gate R]
                (turn targets)
            (R) receiver, (D) defender on the back
                  |
                  |  about 10 m
              [SERVER] plays into R's feet
```

- The server about 10 m from the receiver, the receiver facing the server, the defender behind the receiver.
- Two gates about 3 m wide behind the receiver, one on each side, so the turn can go either way. All the sizes are suggestions.

**One rep:**

1. The server plays the ball into the receiver's feet.
2. The receiver chooses: **turn** away from the defender and carry out through a gate, or **protect and set**, shielding the ball and laying it back to the server, then moving for the return.
3. The rep ends on a gate reached (a point), a clean set back (a point), the defender winning the ball, or the ball out.
4. Rotate receiver, defender and server every 4 reps.

Protecting the ball and setting it back when the turn is not on is a good answer, scored the same as a turn.

**The first block:** 4 reps as receiver, then rotate; three or four rotations. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the duels get slower.

**Load:**

- Pair players by size and maturity, not by age or level alone. The defender may lean in with the body for position and goes for the ball, never kicks through the back of the receiver's legs; a bout ends within a few seconds, and the rotation gives the rest.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** know before the ball comes whether the turn is on.

**Easier or harder:** easier, a defender who stands behind without challenging. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a game problem for three: a server, a receiver and a defender. The choice it trains is the receiver's: turn or protect and set, read from where the defender is and where the space is.

It does not train receiving in open space or the run that gets the receiver free of the defender before the ball. [The Directional First Touch](/training/directional-first-touch) trains the touch in open space.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** the defender stands behind without challenging. The receiver finds the touch that opens away and turns to a gate.
- **Level 2:** the defender leans and stays tight on one side but does not tackle. The receiver reads the side and turns away from it, or protects.
- **Level 3:** the defender defends fully. Turn or set is a real choice, and both score.
- **Level 4:** after playing the ball in, the server runs to stand in one gate, choosing which as the ball travels. The receiver reads which gate is open, or sets back if neither turn is on.
- **Level 5:** the server varies the height and weight of the ball, and now and then a second defender steps in from the side.

With four, the fourth waits as the next receiver and steps in as Level 5's second defender; with three, stay at Levels 1 to 4.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The look before the ball.** Does the receiver check over the shoulder before the ball arrives, or receive blind? If they skip it, ask after the rep what they knew about the defender.
- **Calm with the defender on the back.** Deliberate, or rushed the moment they feel the contact?
- **The first touch.** Does it open into space for the turn, or shield the ball for the set? The touch shows the choice.

**One question, between reps:** "Where was the defender, and how did you know?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet while the ball is moving. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("where's the defender?"), never whether to turn.

**Praise** the read and the calm, including the set back: "They were all over you, so you shielded it and set it. Not every ball is a turn."

**Leave for later:** the polish of the turn. First the choice and the calm; the turn itself sharpens once the choice is reliably right.

**When to simplify:** if the receiver loses the ball on most reps, go back to Level 1 or Level 2.

## Why we think it helps

With a defender pressing from behind, a player can panic: turn into the defender and lose the ball, or knock it backwards blind. This drill gives the receiver many of those moments in a short time, with two answers that both score, so the receiver learns to feel the pressure and choose rather than escape. Level 2's defender picks the side, so the turn goes both ways; a player who can turn either way has more options with a defender on the back. If a player chooses well here and still panics in games, put the same picture into a small-sided game with a target player.

## Self-assessment

- "How did you know whether to turn or to protect? What told you?"
- "When you lost it, was it the read or the touch?"
- "Which way is harder for you to turn, and what does that tell you to practise?"
