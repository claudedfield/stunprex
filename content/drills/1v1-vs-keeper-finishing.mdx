For a coach with two to four players and a keeper. An attacker runs at goal alone against the keeper and has to read what the keeper is doing, then choose the finish: shoot early, go round, or lift it over.

## Quick start

**You need:** a goal and a keeper, 6 cones, several balls, and about 25 m of pitch in front of the goal.

**Set it out:**

```
              [GOAL] + keeper
            +--------------+
            |              |
            |  the keeper  |
            |  starts on   |
            |  the line    |
            |              |
        (A) the attacker starts with the ball, about 20 m out
```

- A start cone about 20 m from goal in the middle, and two more at an angle on each side for later levels. The distances are suggestions.
- The keeper starts on the line and chooses each time, without saying, whether to come out, stay, or show a side.

**One rep:**

1. The attacker goes when ready and runs at goal, reading the keeper from the first stride.
2. The attacker chooses the finish that fits what the keeper is doing, and commits to it.
3. The rep ends on a goal, a save, the ball out, or the keeper holding it.
4. Walk back along the side. The next attacker goes once the keeper is set.

Rotate attackers and, if more than one player keeps goal, keepers. Count good reads, not only goals: a well-read finish the keeper saves is still a good choice.

**The first block:** 4 runs each, then 3 minutes easy; two or three blocks. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the runs get slower.

**Load:**

- Each run is a short burst at full effort, with the walk back before the next and the rest above after each block; stop when the runs get slower and sloppier, and never use extra runs as a punishment.
- The keeper faces about 6 runs, then rests or swaps; with one keeper, shorten the attackers' blocks to match.
- The keeper goes down on a soft surface with the hands to the ball, and any knock to the head stops play for that player. Once the keeper has the hands on the ball, the attacker stops: no kick at a ball the keeper is holding or smothering.
- A chaser, at the levels that add one, goes for the ball from the side, never into the back. Pair players by size and maturity, not by age or level alone.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** decide before you get there.

**Easier or harder:** easier, a keeper who stays on the line. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a game problem with one attacker and a keeper. The choice it trains is the finish, read from what the keeper is doing.

It does not train beating an outfield defender or the run that makes the chance. [The Breakaway](/training/breakaway-finish-fatigue) adds a chaser from behind over a longer carry.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** the keeper stays on the line. The attacker reads the distance and chooses shoot or go round.
- **Level 2:** the keeper chooses each time whether to come out or stay. The attacker reads it live.
- **Level 3:** starts from the angled cones. The angle takes away the easy near-post shot, so the choice changes.
- **Level 4:** a chaser starts 3 m behind and goes on the attacker's first touch, so the attacker has two things to read at once.
- **Level 5:** a teammate plays the ball through from about 30 m for the attacker to run onto, with the keeper fully live and the chaser behind; at most two touches inside the box.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **When the choice is made.** Before the attacker reaches the keeper, or on top of them?
- **The fit.** Did the finish fit what the keeper did? A finish that did not fit is a read to look at, not a technique to fix.
- **Calm.** A placed finish, or a hurried blast?

**One question, between reps:** "What was the keeper doing, and what did that leave you?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet while the ball is moving. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("out or back?"), never which finish to choose.

**Praise** the read and the calm, whether it went in or not: "You saw the keeper come and went round. Save or not, that was the right picture."

**Leave for later:** the move used to go round the keeper. Let the attacker find their own way; the drill asks for a finish that fits the read, not a set move.

**When to simplify:** if most reps end with a rushed shot, go back to Level 1 and move the start closer.

## Why we think it helps

Alone against the keeper, a player who has not decided tends to wait, let the keeper set, and shoot into them. In this drill the keeper changes what they do each time, so the attacker cannot script the finish and has to read and choose. It is also a calm-under-pressure drill: the attacker gets many of these moments in a short time, and each missed one is worth a question about the read rather than about the result. A finish with the weaker foot counts the same when it fits the read, because a player who can finish with either foot has more options at an angle. If a player reads well here and still rushes in games, put the same moment into a small-sided game with a long pitch.

## Self-assessment

- "What did you read in the keeper on your best finish?"
- "When you missed, was it the choice or the strike?"
- "How did it feel to be one against one? What helps you stay calm there?"
