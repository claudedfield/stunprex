For a coach with three to six players. Players pass to each other through gates scattered in a square, and a pass counts only when it goes through a gate to a teammate who is moving. Soon a light defender tries to touch the ball. Each receiver has to find the next open gate and a free teammate, and take a first touch that goes toward them. Every player takes the defender's role.

## Quick start

**You need:** about 12 markers, 1 or 2 balls, and a square about 20 m by 20 m.

**Set it out:**

```
   +-------------------------------+
   |   [ ]            [ ]          |
   |          [ ]                  |   6 gates, each about
   |   [ ]          [ ]       [ ]  |   1.5 m wide, at
   |                               |   different angles
   +-------------------------------+
            about 20 m x 20 m
```

- A square about 20 m by 20 m with 6 gates, each two markers about 1.5 m apart, set at different angles. All sizes are suggestions.

**How it runs:**

1. Players pass to each other through the gates and follow their pass, ready to be the next option.
2. A pass counts when it goes cleanly through a gate to a teammate who is moving. No gate twice in a row.
3. Part A: no defender. Part B: one player becomes a light defender (D), who tries to touch the ball and stays on their feet.
4. D swaps every 90 seconds, so everyone defends.
5. Players count their own gate passes in a block, to compare with their own count next time.

**The first block:** about 3 minutes, then 1 to 2 minutes easy; three or four blocks in all. These numbers are a suggestion.

**Load:**

- Young bands (9 to 12): blocks of about 3 minutes and rest after each; D at walking pace or half pace through Level 3.
- Repeated sprints: short runs between gates; rest between blocks, and nobody defends an extra spell as a punishment.
- Contact: D plays the ball, no slide tackles, no tackle from behind.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** know where the next open gate is before the ball reaches you.

**Easier or harder:** easier, bigger gates and no defender. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

The choice this drill trains is where the receiver plays next, which gate and to whom, and so where the first touch goes; the task fixes the gates and the rule and leaves the gate, the teammate and the foot open. Once D comes in, that choice also has to go away from D.

It does not train receiving with a defender right behind. [Turn Out of Trouble](/training/receive-turn-back-pressure) trains that.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** Part A, no defender, at a pace players control.
- **Level 2:** two touches at most: one to open, one to pass.
- **Level 3:** Part B, one light defender.
- **Level 4:** one touch when it is on; two still allowed when it is not. The receiver decides before the ball arrives.
- **Level 5:** two defenders, and every second pass is played or received with the foot each player uses less.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The look before the ball.** Does the receiver tend to look around before the ball arrives, or only after it is at the feet?
- **The first touch.** Does it go toward the next open gate and away from D, or stop under the feet?
- **The choice.** Does the receiver find an open gate and a free teammate, or play the same pass each time?
- **The movement.** After passing, do players move to be the next option, or stand?
- **The foot.** Does the player use the foot the pass needs, or turn back onto one foot and lose time?

**One question, between blocks:** "When did your first touch make the next pass easy, and what did it do?"

**During a block:** stay quiet while the ball is moving. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("where's free?"), never which gate or which teammate.

**Praise** the look and the touch: "You knew where the next gate was before the ball came, and your touch took you there."

**Leave for later:** the weight of every pass. The look and the open touch come first.

**When to simplify:** if D wins the ball almost every time, go back to Level 1 or 2, or make the gates bigger, until passes go through again.

## Why we think it helps

A pass and a first touch are one action seen from two ends: the touch decides whether the next pass is on. This drill makes every receiver find the next open gate and a free teammate, then adds a defender so the choice has to change with where the defender is. Players get many receptions in a short time, each a little different. Level 5 trains the foot a player uses less with deliberate practice, because a player who can use either foot has more options. If players combine well here and not in games, put the same rule (a pass counts only to a teammate on the move) into a small-sided game and see whether the look before the ball is still there.

## Self-assessment

- "When did your first touch make the next pass easy?"
- "How did the defender change the way you received?"
- "Which foot needs more work after today, and how did you know?"
