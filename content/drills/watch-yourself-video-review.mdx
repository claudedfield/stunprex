For a player of seventeen and over. In a match you see only part of your own game. Footage lets you watch it from the outside and look at what you chose, what else was on, and what you missed. This routine keeps a review short and specific: one thing to look for, the options at each involvement, and one thing to train next.

## Quick start

**You need:** footage of your own play (a match, a training game, or a few clips) and a notebook or a notes app.

**One review (about 20 to 30 minutes, within a day or two of the match):**

1. **Pick one thing to look for.** Your decisions in possession, your position when defending, your looks before receiving. One, not all of them.
2. **At each involvement, pause.** What options did I have? Which did I choose? Was another one on? The choice is the subject, not whether it came off.
3. **Note what came before the choice:** the look before the ball arrived, the body shape, where the space was.
4. **End with one thing to train.** One or two specific things for the next sessions, taken from what the footage showed.
5. **Write it down** with the date, the thing you looked for and the thing you will train.

**Load:** no physical load. Short reviews, a few a month. The review sits beside training and never takes its place; if reviewing starts to replace sessions on the pitch, watch less.

**One thing to think about:** what did I see on the footage that I could not see in the match?

## What this drill is, and what it is not

The choice this routine trains is the player's own read of their decisions: what was on, what they chose, and what to train next. The routine fixes one thing to look for and the questions; the judgment is the player's.

It is not a rating of the player. Nothing is scored against anyone else, and the record is compared only with the player's own earlier records. It is not a highlight reel either: a good-looking moment and a good choice are not always the same thing.

**The footage:** it is yours and stays with you (and your family, if you live at home). Clips that show other players are not posted or shared.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** one thing to look for, every review. The habit is watching for something rather than re-watching.
- **Level 2:** at each involvement, name the options before judging the choice.
- **Level 3:** note what came before each choice (the look, the body shape) as well as how it ended.
- **Level 4:** each review ends with one thing to train, and the next review checks the footage for it.
- **Level 5:** the player runs the review and the training that follows on their own across a season.

## For a coach, or for yourself

**Watch for:**

- A review with one thing to look for, or a passive re-watch of the highlights.
- Choices and options studied, or only goals and mistakes.
- A specific next thing to train, or a general feeling.

**Questions to ask yourself:** "What am I watching for?" "What were my options there; was another one on?" "What came before that choice?" "What one thing does this send me to train?"

**A note worth writing:** "I stop looking around when I'm tired in the second half. That's the next thing to train."

**Leave for later:** technique you see on the footage, unless it is the one thing you chose to look for.

**If a pass came off, or failed:** ask whether the choice was good, separately from whether it came off.

**What not to do:** compare your footage with another player's, give yourself a mark, or review instead of training.

## Why we think it helps

A player who reads their own choices on footage, with the options in view, can see patterns that are hard to feel during a match, and the next sessions can be aimed at them. We keep the record to the player's own earlier reviews, because the useful question is whether the same choices are getting better, not how they compare with anyone else. If the reviews start to feel like a verdict on the player rather than a look at choices, keep them shorter and less frequent.

## Self-assessment

After a review, and again after a few weeks of reviews:

- "What did the footage show about my choices that I could not feel in the moment?"
- "Did I look at the choices, or only the results?"
- "Is the thing I trained after the last review showing up on this footage?"
