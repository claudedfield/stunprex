For a player alone, or two to four taking turns and pairing up. The player moves up a zigzag of cones in a low, side-on stance, changing direction without crossing the feet; then a partner with the ball walks or jogs down the lane and the player stays in front of them, choosing which way to show them.

## Quick start

**You need:** 6 to 8 cones, 1 ball, and a strip about 10 m long and 4 m wide.

**Set it out:**

```
   o           o
       o   o            cones about 1.5 m apart,
   o           o        zigzag left and right
       o   o            down a lane about 10 m long
   o           o
  START
```

- 6 to 8 cones zigzag left and right down a lane about 10 m long, about 1.5 m apart. The sizes are suggestions.

**Part A, the cone path (alone):**

1. The player starts at the bottom, side-on, knees bent, weight forward on the feet.
2. The player moves up the lane to each cone in turn, changing direction at each one with a drop-step, the feet shuffling rather than crossing.
3. The aim is the stance, not the speed: low and balanced, able to push off either way at any moment.

**Part B, the shadow (pairs):**

1. A partner walks the ball down the lane, changing direction when they choose.
2. The defender stays in front at about 2 m, side-on, and shows the partner towards one side.
3. No tackle. The defender's job is to stay in front, low and balanced, for the length of the lane, and to choose which way to show.
4. Swap roles after each length.

**The first block:** efforts of 30 to 40 seconds, then 30 seconds rest; about 6 efforts, then 2 minutes easy; two blocks. These numbers are a suggestion.

**Load:**

- The efforts are short and controlled; the rest above comes after each one, and nobody does extra lengths as a punishment. No level is built on being tired.
- From Level 5, pair players by size and maturity, not by age or level alone; the defender contains and does not tackle.
- For 9 to 12: about 15 minutes, ending while they still want more; the first block's numbers or fewer.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** low, and the feet do not cross.

**Easier or harder:** easier, walking pace and fewer cones. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a technique drill with a choice in it at its later levels. The technique is the defender's stance and footwork. The choice is which way to show the attacker, read from how the partner moves.

It does not train when to go for the ball. [Delay, Deny, Decide](/training/delay-deny-decide-1v1-defending) trains that.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** the cone path at walking pace. The stance and the feet only.
- **Level 2:** the cone path at a jog. The stance has to hold at a higher pace.
- **Level 3:** the shadow with a partner walking and changing direction.
- **Level 4:** the partner jogs and adds body feints. The defender watches the hips, not the ball.
- **Level 5:** the partner becomes a live attacker trying to carry over a line behind the defender; the defender contains for about 8 seconds without lunging. Keeping the attacker in front is the job, not winning the ball.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The feet.** Do they shuffle, or cross on the change of direction?
- **The height.** Knees bent and hips low the whole way, or standing up as the pace rises?
- **The angle.** Side-on, showing one way, or square to the attacker?
- **The eyes.** In the shadow, on the partner's hips, or on the ball?
- **The gap.** About 2 m, or too close to react, or so far the attacker can turn?

**One question, between efforts:** "Where did your stance break down: the speed, the balance, or the feet?"

**During an effort:** stay quiet. When a player is stuck, a question between efforts may help ("what were their hips doing?"), never which way to show.

**Praise** the quality, not the time: "That was low and balanced the whole way."

**Leave for later:** the speed of the footwork. A slow, correct stance first; speed comes once the shape is reliable.

**When to simplify:** if the stance breaks on most efforts, go back to walking pace on the cone path.

## Why we think it helps

A defender who stands square and tall finds it hard to change direction, and a lunge often follows. The stance is one part of defending well; the read is the other. This drill builds the stance first on a path the player can repeat, then asks for it against a partner who changes direction, so the footwork is there when the player has to read a real attacker. If a player holds the stance here and still lunges in games, move on to Delay, Deny, Decide, where the moment to go for the ball is the choice.

## Self-assessment

- "Where did your stance break down today?"
- "In the shadow, could you tell which way your partner was going before they went? What told you?"
- "Which way did you show your partner, and why that way?"
