## Quick start

**You need:** a ball, 21 markers and a phone or a watch for the rests. Cones, shoes and bottles all work as markers. You need flat ground about 25 m long and 9 m wide, and a few metres more behind the finish gate, or a fence or wall there, to stop the ball. You run it alone, and the runs are not timed. Setting it out takes about five minutes the first time.

**Which foot:** the one you use less. If you are right-footed, that is your left.

**Set it out,** in one line from the start:

```
        finish gate (2 m wide)
              •     •
                 ↑   6 m
        turn box (3 m × 3 m)
              •     •
              •     •
                 ↑   2 m
        gate maze (about 8 m × 8 m): 5 gates, turned different ways
           • •        • •
                 • •
           • •        • •
                 ↑   the maze begins at the last slalom marker
        slalom: 5 markers, 1 m apart
                 •
                 •
                 •
                 •
                 •
                 ↑   2 m
              [start]
```

- **Slalom:** 5 markers one behind the other, 1 m apart, in the direction you run. The first is 2 m from the start.
- **Gate maze:** 5 gates. Each gate is two markers 1.5 m apart. Scatter them over about 8 m × 8 m and point them different ways. The maze begins at the last slalom marker.
- **Turn box:** a square of 3 m × 3 m, 2 m beyond the maze.
- **Finish gate:** 2 m wide, 6 m beyond the box.

**The one rule:** only your weaker foot touches the ball. When the other foot touches it, that is a "rescue touch". You lose nothing. Say the number out loud ("one", "two") and keep going.

**One run:**

1. **Slalom.** Weave in and out of the five markers.
2. **Maze.** Take the ball through any three of the five gates, choosing as you go. Do not use the same three in the same order as on your last run.
3. **Turn box.** Go in, make one turn (a pull-back, an inside cut or an outside hook) and come out through a different side.
4. **Finish.** From where you leave the box, pass or strike the ball through the finish gate.
5. **Fetch the ball and walk back** to the start with it, still using only the weaker foot. Touches on the walk back are not part of the count.

**When it goes wrong:**

- You hit a marker: carry on.
- The ball runs away: fetch it with the weaker foot, bring it back to where it left the course and carry on. Touches with the other foot on the way count as rescue touches.
- You miss the finish gate: there is no second try. Fetch the ball and start the next run.

**The first block:** 5 runs at a comfortable pace, then 90 seconds rest. The walk back is your rest between runs. Stop if something hurts, or if you are tired and the touches are getting worse.

**One thing to think about:** small touches. A slow, clean run is worth more than a quick, messy one.

**Easier or harder:** to make it easier, take out the second and fourth slalom markers and use any two gates. To make it harder, move the five gates to new places before the next block.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a supporting drill for practising on your own. It gives your weaker foot the touches it does not get in a game, where you can often turn onto the other one.

The one real choice in it is your route through the maze. It does not teach you which foot to use in a game, and there is no opponent. In a game you do not get to choose which side the ball arrives on. [First Rondo](/training/3v1-rondo-foundation), for four players, is a drill where that happens.

For children of five to eight there is a gentler version of this drill, [Adventure Trail](/training/adventure-trail).

## A full session

- Three blocks of five runs, with 90 seconds rest between blocks. That is 15 runs. We have not timed a session, so allow about 25 minutes the first time.
- Before each run, say which of the three turns you will make. Use all three in a block.
- After each block, write down two things for every run, on paper or a phone: the rescue touches, and whether the finish went through the gate. For example: 4, 6, 3, 5, 2, and three finishes of five. If you forget them by then, write after each run, on the walk back.
- A run with no rescue touch and a clean finish is a clean run.
- Read your numbers across sessions, not inside one. Compare them only with your own earlier numbers. They say nothing about anybody else.
- **Two players** can share one course: take turns, one run each. The second starts when the first has finished, and each walks back around the outside. The session then takes about twice as long, or each player does two blocks. With more players, build a second course.
- **On less space,** about 17 m × 6 m is enough, with the same room behind the finish gate: slalom markers 80 cm apart with the first 1 m from the start, a maze of 5 m × 5 m with four gates (take any three of the four; 19 markers in all), the box 1 m beyond it and 2.5 m square, and the finish gate 4 m beyond the box.
- **When you have finished,** ask yourself which part of the course cost you the most rescue touches, and start there next time.

## What good looks like

- In the slalom you use the inside and the outside of the foot, and now and then the sole.
- In the maze your head comes up to pick the next gate.
- In the box the turn is finished and you are balanced when you come out.
- At the finish the ball goes through the gate. A rolled ball through it is better than a hard one past it.
- You count every rescue touch, including the ones you would rather not count.

## Ways to change it

Stay with the base version until more than half the runs in a session are clean. Then try one of these. You do not have to take them in order.

- **A new maze (harder).** Move the five gates to new places in every rest. A route you remembered no longer works, so you have to find a new one. Leave the maze alone for a session if you keep running into gates.
- **Another ball (different, not harder).** Use a different ball for each block: a normal one, a smaller or futsal ball, a slightly soft one. With one ball, do one session on grass and the next on a hard surface. The count may go up the first time. That is the ball, not you. Go back to your usual ball if the runs turn messy.
- **Eyes up (a short check).** A helper stands beside the finish gate, or as near as the side of the turn box if the fingers are hard to see, and, now and then while you are in the maze, holds up one to five fingers. Say the number without stopping the ball. Afterwards the helper says how many you caught. This only checks whether your head comes up. A number of fingers is nothing you would look for in a game, so use this for one block and then leave it out again.
- **Cards (harder).** Write one rule on each of four cards: "outside of the foot only, in the slalom and the maze"; "sole only in the slalom"; "no more than two touches between gates"; "count in your head and say the total at the end". Draw one before each run. Go back to runs without cards if the cards turn every run into a messy one.
- **Against your own time (harder).** Only once clean runs are normal for you: time a clean run, then try to match it and stay clean. Your own time is the only one that counts. Drop the watch if the rescue touches come back.

## For a parent or coach

The drill runs without you. If you are there, this is what you can add.

**Watch for:**

- whether the count is honest. A player who leaves rescue touches out may be hearing the count as a mark against them, or may just have lost count. Say that it is a measurement. If it says six, six is where today starts;
- the same route through the maze on every run;
- the same turn every time, usually the pull-back;
- speed going up and the count going up with it. Nobody is timing the base version.

**Questions to ask:**

- "Which part of the foot was that?"
- "Which gates have you not used today?"
- "Which part of the course is costing you the most rescue touches?"

**Leave alone for now:** a slow pace, awkward touches with the outside of the foot, and the fine points of the turn. Let the player find them first.

**After a run with a high count,** ask one question and let the next run start: "What was your count the first time you did this, and what is it today?" If there is no earlier count yet, or today's is higher, ask instead which part of the course cost the most.

**If you praise,** praise an honest count or a new route, not a fast run.

**Make it easier, or stop, when:**

- the layout or the rules confuse the player: go back to the slalom and two gates (15 markers, and a fair way to start at home);
- the count stays high run after run and nothing changes: take out the second and fourth slalom markers;
- the player has had enough: finish for today;
- something hurts: stop.

## Why we think it helps

This is our reasoning, and we have not tested it. In a game you can often avoid your weaker foot, so it gets little practice there. Here it gets every touch, in private, and the count shows where the other foot still steps in.

**Where it should show later:** in a small game or a rondo, the ball arrives on your weaker side and you take it with that foot, where before you would have turned onto the other one.

**What would count against it:** your runs here get clean, and in games you still turn onto your stronger foot every time.
