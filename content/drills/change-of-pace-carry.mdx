For a player practising alone, or up to four taking turns. You carry the ball down a channel through four gates at uneven distances: easy through the two close ones, a burst across the long gap, and slow again in time for the last gate.

## Quick start

**You need:** a ball, 8 markers for the gates (12 if you also mark the corners of the channel), and flat ground about 30 m long and 7 m wide, with a few metres free beyond the end. Setting it out takes a few minutes.

**Set it out** along the channel, measured from the start line:

```
start                                                          end
  |------ G1 --- G2 ------------------ G3 ------ G4 ----------|
  0 m     6 m    10 m                  21 m      26 m        30 m
```

- Each gate is two markers 3 m apart, across the middle of the channel.
- Gates 1 and 2 are 4 m apart: you glide through them. From gate 2 to gate 3 is 11 m: that is where you burst. Gate 4 comes 5 m after gate 3, so you have to slow down in time. All distances are suggestions.

**One run:**

1. Start on the start line with the ball. Carry it through gates 1 and 2 at an easy pace, ball close, head up so you can see gate 3.
2. After gate 2, pick your moment and burst: push the ball further ahead and speed up.
3. Slow down before gate 4 so the ball goes through it under control.
4. Carry on to the end line at whatever pace you like, stop the ball on the line, and walk back along the side of the channel with it. The walk back is your rest.

**When it goes wrong:**

- The ball runs away on the burst: fetch it, bring it back to where it left the channel and carry on.
- You miss a gate: carry on to the next one. On the next run, burst a little later or push the ball a little softer.

**With more than one player:** the next player starts when the one ahead has gone through gate 3, so two players never burst in the same space. Everyone walks back down the same side.

**The first block:** 6 runs, then 2 minutes rest; two or three blocks in all. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the runs get worse.

**Load:**

- Each burst is short and at full effort, and the walk back comes before the next one. Stop when the runs get slower and sloppier, and never use extra runs as a punishment. No level is built on being tired.
- For players of nine to twelve: about 15 minutes, ending while they still want more; the first block's numbers or fewer. Watch the slowing down as well as the burst: a player who starts to stumble when slowing has done enough for today.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** a real difference between the glide and the burst, with the ball still close enough to reach.

**Easier or harder:** to make it easier, make the gates 4 m wide and walk the glide. To make it harder, move gates 2 and 3 to new places before the next block, so the long gap is somewhere new and you have to find it (keep at least 3 m between gates).

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a supporting drill: it practises one thing, changing speed with the ball, without an opponent. The one choice in it is the moment of the burst.

It does not teach you when a change of pace beats a defender, because nobody is in front of you. [Slow to Fast](/training/change-of-pace-1v1) puts the same change of pace against a live defender.

## Ways to change it

- **New spacing each block:** as in "harder" above. The player has to see where the long gap is before choosing where to burst.
- **A partner in the gap:** a partner stands in the long gap. As the player comes out of gate 2, the partner takes one step to one side; the player bursts past on the other side. The partner chooses the side and the moment, never the same twice in a row, and does not tackle. This is still a supporting drill: the partner gives the player something to see, not a duel. Swap roles after each block.

## In a field of cones

The same change of speed works in a crowded space instead of a channel. Scatter about 20 markers loosely over a square about 15 m by 15 m (both numbers are suggestions), leaving some lanes open and some tight. The player carries through it at a glide, head up, looking for an open lane; when one opens ahead, they burst along it and slow down before the next tight spot. Nobody says where the lanes are: the cones show them, and the lane changes with where the player is.

This is a condition on this drill, not a drill of its own: the cones change the space and the route, and the choice is still the moment of the burst, now with the head up to find where. It works for a player alone. It does not train reading an opponent; for that, go to Slow to Fast. The same load lines hold, and it is a good way to end a session while the player still wants more.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:** a real contrast between the glide and the burst; the ball still reachable during both; the player balanced when slowing for gate 4; in the field of cones, the head up between touches.

**One question, between runs:** "Where did you choose to burst, and what made you go there?"

**During a run:** stay quiet while the ball is moving; the gates show whether it worked. When the player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("where's the long gap?"), never where to burst.

**When to simplify:** if the ball runs away on most bursts, or the player stops changing speed at all, widen the gates and move gate 3 to 18 m so the long gap is shorter.

## Why we think it helps

In a game, a player who carries the ball at one speed is easy to stay with; a change of speed is often what opens the gap. This drill packs many speed changes with the ball into a short time, so the touch on the burst and on the slow-down can settle before the player tries it on an opponent. If the bursts look good here but never show up against a defender, the next step is Slow to Fast or a small-sided game, not more runs down this channel.

## Self-assessment

- "Which burst kept the ball closest, and what did you do differently on that one?"
- "Compared with last time, are you slowing down for gate 4 earlier or later?"
