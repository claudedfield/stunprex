For a coach with three to six players and, ideally, a keeper. One player beats a defender and then has to choose what to do with the chance: shoot, pass to a teammate making a run, or cut it back, read from where the cover and the keeper are.

## Quick start

**You need:** a goal with a keeper (or two mini-goals), 6 markers, several balls, bibs, and a space about 20 m wide and 25 m long in front of the goal.

**Set it out:**

```
              [GOAL] + keeper
        +--------------------------+
        |  (D2) starts here,       |
        |   to one side            |
        |          (D1)       (R)  |
        |                          |
        |          (A) with ball   |
        +--------------------------+
   area about 20 m wide and 25 m long
```

- Four markers for the corners of an area about 20 m wide and 25 m long in front of the goal. Sizes are suggestions.
- One marker about 15 m from goal for D1, and one about 8 m to the side of it, a little nearer goal, for D2. A starts about 8 m in front of D1; R starts wide on the other side.

**One rep:**

1. A goes when ready and takes on D1, with any move.
2. D2 may go the moment A touches the ball, and comes across to cover. R makes a run when they choose. R may call their own run ("here", "behind"); nobody calls what A should do.
3. Once past D1, A reads the picture and chooses: shoot, pass to R, or cut back.
4. The rep ends on a goal, a save, the ball going out, or a defender winning it.
5. Everyone walks back and moves one role on: A to R, R to D2, D2 to D1, D1 to A. The keeper swaps in after each block.

**With three:** A, D1 and R; D2 comes in at Level 3 once you have four. **With five or six:** the extra players wait at the start and come in as the next attacker.

**The first block:** 4 attempts each as attacker, then 3 minutes easy (passing, walking, a drink); two or three blocks in all. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the runs get slower.

**Load:**

- Each rep is a short burst at full effort. The walk back and the rotation come before the next one. Stop when the runs get slower and sloppier, and never use extra reps as a punishment.
- Pair players by size and maturity, not by age or level alone. Defenders play for the ball, never into the back or across the legs. The keeper goes down on a soft surface with the hands to the ball, and any knock to the head stops play for that player.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** once you are past, look before you decide.

**Easier or harder:** easier, D1 only shadows and there is no D2. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a game problem with an attacker, one or two defenders, a teammate and a keeper. The choice the player makes is the final action after the beat, read from the cover, the runner and the keeper.

It does not train the beating move itself. [Slow to Fast](/training/change-of-pace-1v1) trains beating a defender with a change of pace.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** D1 shadows and does not tackle, no D2, no R. Beat D1, then shoot.
- **Level 2:** add R. After the beat, shoot or pass.
- **Level 3:** add D2. The final action now comes under pressure.
- **Level 4:** A may take D1 on either side. A reads what each side leaves this time.
- **Level 5:** 2v2 plus the keeper: R and A against D1 and D2, all live from the start. A may beat a defender or combine with R. The contact line above holds for everyone.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The look after the beat.** Does A lift the head once past, or dribble on into the cover?
- **The choice.** Does the final action change with where the cover and the runner are, or is it the same every time?
- **The quality.** Is the action calm, or rushed because the choice came late?

**One question, between reps:** "When you got past, what did you see, and what did it leave you?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("head up once you're past"), never which action to choose.

**Praise** the read and the choice, whatever the result: "You saw the cover come across and found the runner. That was the right ball, even though it was saved."

**Leave for later:** the beating move. Let A beat D1 any way they like; the work here is what comes after.

**When to simplify:** if most reps end with A dribbling into D2, go back to Level 2 and let A take one rep where the only task is to lift the head once past, then add the choice back.

## Why we think it helps

Beating a defender is worth something in a game when it leads to a chance. This drill puts a choice after every beat, with cover coming and a teammate running, so the player learns to look once they are past. We judge a rep by the choice and its quality: a well-chosen pass that is saved counts for more than a lucky shot. A broken rep is worth one question about what broke (the look, the choice or the action). If the player chooses well here and still dribbles into trouble in games, put the same picture into a small-sided game with a keeper.

## Self-assessment

- "After you beat your defender, what did you see? Did you choose what it left you?"
- "When did a shot waste a better pass, or a pass waste a better shot?"
- "What helps you look up the moment you are past someone?"
