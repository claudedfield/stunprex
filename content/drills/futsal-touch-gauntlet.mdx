For a player alone, or up to four taking turns, in a sports hall or on a futsal court. This is the [Weak-Foot Dribbling Gauntlet](/training/weak-foot-dribbling-gauntlet) on a hard floor with a futsal ball: four short zones, every touch with the weaker foot, and the player finds out which touches and turns this ball and this floor reward.

## Quick start

**You need:** a futsal ball (size 4, low-bounce), about 14 markers, and about 16 m by 10 m of hall floor.

**Set it out:**

```
          [finish gate]  2 markers, about 1.5 m apart
                |   about 5 m
          [turn box]     4 markers, about 2.5 m square
                |
          [gate maze]    4 gates of 2 markers, about 1.2 m wide
                |
          [slalom]       5 markers, about 80 cm apart
          [start]
```

- About 14 to 16 m from start to finish gate and 8 to 10 m wide. Every size is a suggestion; shrink them in a small hall.

**One run:**

1. **Slalom:** dribble through the five markers with the weaker foot.
2. **Gate maze:** go through any 3 of the 4 gates, choosing the route on the move, never the same gate twice in a run.
3. **Turn box:** go in, turn, and come out through a different side from the one you entered.
4. **Finish:** from behind the 5 m line, roll or drive the ball through the finish gate.
5. Count the touches taken with the stronger foot (the rescue touches) and walk back to the start with the ball.

**The one rule:** every touch with the weaker foot. A stronger-foot touch is allowed when the ball would otherwise be lost; it is counted, not punished.

**The first block:** 5 runs, then 90 seconds easy; three blocks. With two to four players, the next starts when the one ahead leaves the slalom. These numbers are a suggestion.

**Load:**

- **The floor:** indoor shoes with flat, non-marking soles, never studs or blades; a dry, clean floor, swept of anything loose; walls and benches at least a few metres from the finish gate. The hall's own rules come first.
- The runs are short and at a pace the player controls; nobody does extra runs as a punishment.
- For 9 to 12: about 15 minutes, ending while they still want more; the first block's numbers or fewer.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** this ball does not slow down on its own.

**Easier or harder:** easier, wider gates and a bigger turn box. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a technique drill on a new surface, with a choice in it: the route through the maze, and which touch and turn work on this floor. The same course outdoors is the base gauntlet; this page changes only the ball and the floor.

It does not train beating a defender. A one against one in the hall does that.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** the layout stays the same for all three blocks.
- **Level 2:** move the maze gates between blocks, so no route can be learned by heart.
- **Level 3:** the four maze gates narrowed to about 1 m wide.
- **Level 4:** if there is a second ball, a standard ball with a little air let out, swap balls between blocks.
- **Level 5:** draw a constraint card before each run, as in the base gauntlet (sole touches only in the slalom, or the zones in reverse order), and time the runs against the player's own best on this floor.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The slalom.** Heavy touches that run into the next marker. Does the touch get lighter across the blocks?
- **The sole.** Does the player find the sole to slow the ball on the smooth floor, or keep using a touch that worked on grass?
- **The turn.** An outside hook can slide on a hard floor. Does the player keep trying the same turn, or try another?
- **The count.** The rescue count often goes up on the first session on a new ball and floor. That is the new surface, not a step back.

**One question, between blocks:** "How does the ball feel different on this floor, and what did you change?"

**During a run:** stay quiet. When a player is stuck on the same turn, a question between runs may help ("which part of the foot grips this floor?"), never which turn to use.

**Praise** the change the player found: "You slowed it with the sole in the maze. You found that yourself."

**Leave for later:** the finish technique. Let the count settle first.

**When to simplify:** if the count is high on most runs, widen the gates to about 1.5 m and go back to Level 1.

## Why we think it helps

A futsal ball on a hard floor rolls faster and bounces less than a standard ball on grass, so touches that worked outside need adjusting. Running the same course on a new surface asks the player to notice the difference and change the touch, rather than repeat what worked somewhere else. We train the weak foot with deliberate practice, because a player who can use either foot has more options. If the player is comfortable here and still avoids the weaker foot in hall games, play one against one in the hall and count the weaker-foot touches there.

## Self-assessment

- "Which touch worked better on this floor than on grass?"
- "Which turn felt safest here, and why?"
- "How did your count change from the first block to the last?"
