For a coach with six to twelve players. Two teams play a small-sided game, and at moments nobody knows in advance, a rule changes while play goes on: goals count double, everyone goes to two touches, one goal counts more. Each player reads the new rule and works out how to play under it, with no one telling them how.

## Quick start

**You need:** about 8 markers, 2 to 4 mini-goals, bibs, several balls, and a pitch about 30 m by 24 m.

**Set it out:**

```
   [goal]               [goal]
   +-----------------------+
   |                       |
   |   3v3, 4v4 or 5v5     |
   |   about 30 m x 24 m   |
   |                       |
   +-----------------------+
   [goal]               [goal]
        coach at the side, with the joker list
```

- A pitch about 30 m by 24 m, one or two mini-goals at each end. Fit the size to the numbers; all sizes are suggestions.
- Two teams. Spare balls behind each goal so play restarts at once.

**How it runs:**

1. The teams play a normal game.
2. At a moment the coach chooses, the coach calls a joker, once, loud enough for every player at once: "Joker: double goal." The call says only the rule, never how to play under it. Play does not stop.
3. The rule holds for about a minute, or until the coach calls "Joker over."
4. Players do not pass the rule on or tell teammates what to do about it; each player reads it for themselves.

**The jokers (a list for the coach):**

- **Double goal:** for the next minute, goals count double.
- **Touch limit:** two touches each (or one, at Level 4 and above) for a spell.
- **Tilt the pitch:** one goal counts more, or one half becomes a no-dribble zone.
- **Numbers shift:** one player swaps teams for a minute, so one side has an extra player.
- **New ball:** the coach plays a second ball in and calls the first one dead.

**The first block:** about 4 minutes of play, then 2 minutes easy; four or five blocks in all, with one to three jokers a block. These numbers are a suggestion.

**Load:**

- Contact: the normal contact of a small-sided game; no slide tackles, no tackle from behind. With "New ball", the dead ball is left where it is and nobody plays it.
- Repeated sprints: blocks of about 4 minutes with full rest between; nobody plays an extra block as a punishment for losing.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** when the rule changes, ask yourself what it rewards, and play that.

**Easier or harder:** easier, one joker only (double goal) with a few seconds' warning. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

The choice this drill trains is how each player plays once the rule has changed; the call fixes the new rule and leaves the answer open. A joker is a rule change announced to every player at once, and it carries no advice about the play: that part stays with the players.

It does not train a set way to play under each rule. There is often no single right answer, and the drill exists so the players find one.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** one joker (double goal), called with a few seconds' warning. Players get used to a changed rule.
- **Level 2:** jokers called with no warning, in the middle of play.
- **Level 3:** the joker is shown, not called: the coach raises a coloured card at the side of the pitch (red for double goal, yellow for two touches, and so on, explained before the block). Players find the change by looking around. The rule stays the same; only the way it reaches the players changes.
- **Level 4:** two jokers at once (two touches and double goal, for example). Players hold both rules and play under both.
- **Level 5:** each team has one joker card for the game. Any player of that team may play it at a dead ball, announcing the rule to everyone. Players now choose when a rule change helps them, as well as answering the other team's.

**A variation: drawn jokers.** Before each block a player draws a joker card from a pile, face down, and the coach shows it at a moment the coach chooses. Nobody, the coach included, picks the rule in advance.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The change in play.** After a joker, does the team's play change, or does it go on as before?
- **The answer.** Does a player find a way to play that the new rule rewards, or wait for the rule to end?
- **The look.** At Level 3, how soon after the card goes up do players play under the new rule?
- **The mood.** Does a sudden change bring a stop and an argument, or a shrug and play on?

**One question, between blocks:** "When double goal came, what did it reward, and what did you do about it?"

**During play:** stay quiet while the ball is moving, apart from the joker itself. When a player is stuck, a question may help ("what does the rule reward now?"), never what to do under it.

**Praise** the answer a player found, whatever the score: "When it went to two touches you moved to show for the ball earlier. You found that yourself."

**Leave for later:** the best tactical answer to each joker. The players inventing an answer comes first.

**When to simplify:** if a joker stops the game every time, go back to Level 1 with warning until the game flows through a change.

## Why we think it helps

In a match the situation changes all the time, and a player who can read a new situation and find an answer has more to go on than a player who repeats one. This drill makes the change itself the task: the rule shifts, and each player has to read what it rewards and find a way. The answers come from the players, not from the coach, so they are theirs. If players adapt well here and not in games, watch a match for the moments the picture changes (a sending off, a change of score, a new opponent's shape) and see whether they still read it; if they do not, that counts against our reasoning.

## Self-assessment

- "Which rule change was hardest for you, and why?"
- "When you played well under a new rule, what did you see first?"
- "Which joker would you play against this team, and when?"
