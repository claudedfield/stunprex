For a coach or parent with one to three players. You toss the ball up; the player reads where it will drop, gets behind it, and cushions it with the chest, thigh or foot so it dies inside a small square, then plays it out.

## Quick start

**You need:** a ball, 4 markers, and a flat patch of grass about 10 m long. A wall is useful but not needed.

**Set it out:** a square of about 3 m by 3 m. The server stands about 5 m away from it. Both distances are suggestions; move closer if the tosses are not landing.

```
              [square, 3 m x 3 m]
              +-----------------+
              |        P        |
              +-----------------+

                     about 5 m

                        S   (server, underarm toss)
```

**One rep:**

1. The server tosses the ball underarm, up and over, so it drops on the player at chest, thigh or foot height.
2. The player watches the flight, moves to get the body behind the drop, and picks the surface the height asks for.
3. As the ball arrives, the surface gives with it: the chest leans back, the thigh drops away, the foot lowers. The ball should die inside the square.
4. The player settles it and plays it back to the server along the ground.

**When it goes wrong:** the ball bounces out of the square. The player fetches it, plays it back to the server, and the next toss comes. That rep is worth a look, not a telling-off.

**With two or three players:** one works, one serves, one fetches and waits. After a block, rotate: the worker serves, the server fetches, the fetcher works. With one player and no adult, the player tosses the ball up for themselves, or off a wall.

**The first block:** 10 tosses each, then rotate; three or four rounds in all. These numbers are a suggestion: stop earlier if the touches get worse.

**Load:** this is low-load work, but the tosses come quickly. Rest while serving and fetching. Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to. For players of nine to twelve: about 15 minutes, ending while they still want more.

**One thing to think about:** getting the body behind the ball early, so the touch can be soft.

**Easier or harder:** easier, toss lower and always to the same surface. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a supporting drill: it practises one thing, bringing a dropping ball under control, without an opponent. The one choice in it is the surface, made while the ball is in the air.

It does not train heading, and it does not train winning a high ball against someone. Level 5 adds a defender who arrives after the touch, never in the air.

## Heading and load

This drill trains control with the chest, thigh and foot; there is no heading in it. If you add heading later, your football association's rules on heading by age come first, and we give no number of headers. If a ball strikes a player's head and they may have a concussion, stop at once: no more play that day, and a medical check before they return.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** gentle, predictable tosses to one surface (all thigh, for example). The player gets the feel of the give.
- **Level 2:** the server changes the height each toss without saying which. The player reads it and picks chest, thigh or foot.
- **Level 3:** the server sends it to either side as well, without saying which. The player cushions with the side it comes to, not the side they prefer.
- **Level 4:** add two gates of 2 markers each, one either side of the square, about 5 m out. After tossing, the server walks to stand in front of one gate. The player cushions, looks up, and plays out through the other gate. The server changes gates every few tosses, never in a set order.
- **Level 5:** a second player waits about 5 m from the square. As the ball touches the player, the defender comes in to win it. The defender never jumps for the ball and never arrives while it is in the air; they press the player once it is down. The player cushions, protects the ball and plays out through a gate. Pair players by size and maturity, not by age or level alone; contact is for the ball, never into the back; swap roles each block.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The early read.** Does the player get behind the drop, or reach late with one foot?
- **The give.** Does the surface move back as the ball arrives, or stay stiff so the ball bounces off?
- **Both sides.** On Level 3, does the player use the side the ball came to?

**One question, between reps:** "Which surface did that height want, and when did you decide?"

**While the ball is in the air:** stay quiet. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("watch it all the way down"), never the name of the surface to use.

**Praise** what they did: "You got under it early and the thigh dropped away. It died at your feet."

**Leave for later:** the pass after the touch. First the cushion; the play-out sharpens once the touch is reliable.

**When to simplify:** if most balls bounce out, go back a level, toss lower, and make the square bigger.

## Why we think it helps

A high ball is a first touch that comes from above. A player who can kill it dead keeps the ball when the game gets messy, and has a moment to look before the next action. Reading the flight early is the part that carries into a match, so the server's job is to keep the tosses varied once the give is there. If players cushion well here but lose the ball in games, Level 5 and a small-sided game with long passes come next, not more tosses.

## Self-assessment

- "Which surface felt most reliable today, and which needs more work?"
- "On your best cushions, what did your body do as the ball arrived?"
- "Which side is weaker in the air, and how will you practise it?"
