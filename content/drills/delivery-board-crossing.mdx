For a coach with two to five players. One player carries the ball into a wide channel and crosses it; a teammate makes a run into the box, and the crosser chooses the ball that run needs: driven across the near post, pulled back low to the penalty spot, or curled to the far post. Every player takes the crossing role, the running role and, without a keeper, the keeper's, on both sides of the pitch.

## Quick start

**You need:** several balls, about 10 markers, a goal with a keeper if you have one, and about 30 m by 20 m in one corner of the pitch.

**Set it out:**

```
        [GOAL] + keeper
   +-------------------------------+
   |   NEAR       SPOT       FAR   |   three target zones,
   |   [ ]        [ ]        [ ]   |   each about 4 m across
   +-------------------------------+
        (B) runner starts       |
            about 18 m out      |  wide channel,
                                |  about 8 m wide
                             (A) crosser starts deep
```

- Mark a wide channel about 8 m wide along one touchline, from about 30 m out to the byline.
- Mark three target zones in the box, each about 4 m across: near post, penalty spot, far post.
- The runner (B) starts about 18 m out, centrally. All distances are suggestions; fit them to your pitch.

**One rep:**

1. A carries the ball into the channel toward the byline, or takes a pass into it.
2. B makes one run into the box, to whichever zone B chooses, without calling it.
3. A reads the run and crosses the ball that run needs into that zone, off whichever foot the position gives.
4. The rep counts when the ball arrives in the zone B ran to with the type of ball that zone needs: driven along or just above the ground to the near post, low and pulled back to the spot, curled away from the keeper to the far post.
5. B finishes with the foot if the ball arrives at foot height; a ball in the air is caught, or let land in the zone. Nobody heads the ball on this page.
6. Roles move on: A to runner, B to the back of the crossers' line, the next player to the keeper if there is no keeper.

**With two:** A crosses, B runs, no keeper; the zones are the target. **With three to five:** a keeper, and the others take turns. **Both sides:** every second block from the other touchline, so the ball comes off the other foot.

**The first block:** about 8 crosses each, then 2 to 3 minutes easy (passing, walking, a drink); two or three blocks in all. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the crosses get sloppier.

**Load:**

- Striking: about 8 crosses a block, then the rest above; long, driven crosses in small numbers and only once warm; around a growth spurt, pain near a joint or at the front of the hip means stop, not push on.
- The carry and the run are short sprints: walk back between reps.
- No heading: aerial balls are caught or let land. Heading has its own pages and its own load lines.
- With a keeper, swap the keeper every block.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** see where B is going before the last touch, so the cross is chosen, not hoped.

**Easier or harder:** easier, B makes the same run for a block and A works on the one ball it needs. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

The choice this drill trains is which ball to cross for the run a teammate is making; the task fixes the channel and the three zones and leaves the run, the ball and the foot open. The crosser reads the runner and the space, and the runner reads where the space is. Nobody tells either of them.

It does not train beating the defender before the cross. [Beat and Cross](/training/beat-and-cross-wide) trains getting the yard to cross in, and [Arrive Late, Finish Clean](/training/cutback-finish-arriving-late) trains the runner's timing onto a ball pulled back.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** no runner. A carries in and crosses to a zone A has chosen, with the ball that zone needs, and says nothing about it first. The rep counts on the ball arriving in a zone with its type.
- **Level 2:** as Level 1, crossing from different depths: some early from about 25 m out, some from the byline. A finds which balls each depth allows.
- **Level 3:** B runs, as in the rep above, and chooses the zone without calling it. A reads the run and crosses the matching ball.
- **Level 4:** B may change the run late, once, after A's last touch toward the byline. A reads the change, or chooses a ball the first run can still reach.
- **Level 5:** a defender (D) closes A down in the channel to block the cross, standing and blocking only, no slide and no tackle from behind. A finds the yard to cross in and still crosses the ball B's run needs.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The look.** Does A see where B is going before the last touch, or only after the cross is on its way?
- **The type.** Does the ball change with the zone, or is it the same cross every time?
- **The arrival.** Does the ball arrive where B is arriving, or into the space B has already left?
- **The foot.** Does A use the foot the position gives, or shape back onto one foot every time and lose the moment?
- **The cut-back.** From the byline, does it go low and back, or get lifted?

**One question, between reps:** "Where was B going when you took your last touch, and what ball did that run need?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet while the ball is moving. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("where's the run?"), never which zone or which ball.

**Praise** the read and the matching ball, whatever happens to the finish: "You saw B going to the near post early and drove it across. That was the ball for that run."

**Leave for later:** the power of the cross. The right ball to the right place comes first; the pace follows once the type and the arrival hold.

**When to simplify:** if most crosses miss every zone, go back to Level 1 and let A cross from the byline only until the contact is clean again.

## Why we think it helps

A cross is a pass to a teammate who is moving, and which ball it needs depends on where that teammate is going. This drill puts that read in front of the crosser on every rep: the runner chooses, the crosser reads and answers. We count the right ball arriving in the right place, not goals and not power. Crossing from both touchlines trains the cross off either foot, with deliberate practice, because a player who can cross with either foot has more options in the channel. If a player crosses well here and not in games, put crosses into a small-sided game with wide channels and see whether the look before the last touch is still there; if it is not, that counts against our reasoning, and the drill is changed.

## Self-assessment

- "Which run did you read earliest today, and what told you?"
- "Which ball was most reliable for you, and which needs work?"
- "Where did you lose the moment by changing feet?"
