For a parent with two to six children of five to eight, in pairs. One child leads, doing whatever they like with the ball, and the partner copies everything, like a reflection. To keep up, the partner has to watch the leader's whole body, not only the ball. Then they swap. No counting, no winning, no levels.

## Quick start

**You need:** a ball each (or one ball a pair), and an open, safe space about 10 m by 10 m.

**Set it out:**

```
     open space, partners facing each other, about 2 m apart

        (leader, with the ball)   (mirror, with the ball)
```

- Partners face each other about 2 m apart, each with a ball. With one ball a pair, the mirror copies without a ball. The distance is a suggestion.

**How it runs:**

1. The leader moves with the ball: a roll, a step, a stop, a spin. The mirror copies it, as a reflection would.
2. After about a minute, swap who leads. Swap often, so every child invents and every child copies.
3. With more than two, swap partners now and then.

**Load:**

- For 5 to 8: about 10 minutes, ending while they still want more; a drink and a rest whenever they like. Pain is never part of play.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a fall that hurts; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** watch all of your partner, not just the ball.

**Easier:** the leader moves slowly and without the ball first.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is play for young children. The leader chooses every move; the mirror reads the leader's body and adapts to it as it happens.

It is not a race or a test of who copies best.

## How it grows

There are no levels. These are ways the game changes as the children are ready.

- **Slow mirror:** the leader moves slowly with the ball, and the partner matches.
- **Silly moves:** the leader gets inventive (a spin, a wiggle, a hop over the ball), and the mirror copies it all.
- **Surprise freezes:** the leader may freeze at any moment, and the mirror freezes too.
- **Follow the path:** the leader dribbles a winding path around the space and the partner (or a small line of followers) follows it with their own ball. The followers keep up by watching where the leader goes; nobody calls the moves.
- **Their own rules:** the children add twists of their own ("if I clap, we both jump!"). The game becomes theirs.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **Eyes on the partner.** Is the mirror watching the leader's body, or only the ball?
- **Happy faces.** Laughing, copying, inventing.
- **Brave inventing.** A leader trying a wild new move.

**During play:** stay out of the inventing. When a mirror is stuck watching only the ball, a light question may help ("can you see their arms too?"), never what the leader will do next.

**Praise** the watching and the inventing: "You copied that spin!" "Your turn to lead. Show us something new!"

**If the leader goes too fast,** ask them to slow down so their friend can keep up. **If one child always wants to lead,** swap warmly; both halves are the game. **If it gets too serious,** add a surprise freeze or a silly rule.

**What not to do:** make it a competition over who copies best, count anything, or run it long.

## Why we think it helps

To copy a partner, a child has to watch the whole of another person and keep adjusting to what they do, which is part of what reading a player means later. Here it happens as play, and the leader's freedom to invent keeps it fun. Joy is part of what the game is for, not a side effect. If the children love the mirror and do not seem to watch each other in games, leave the games alone: they are theirs.

## Self-assessment

Just one question, only if they want to answer:

- "What was the trickiest thing your partner did that you had to copy?"
