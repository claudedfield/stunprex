For a coach with two to four players and, ideally, a keeper. One player breaks from deep with the ball and a defender chasing from behind, and has to settle near the box and choose a finish from what the keeper is doing.

## Quick start

**You need:** a goal with a keeper (or a mini-goal), 4 markers, several balls, and about 40 m of pitch in front of the goal.

**Set it out:**

```
                 [GOAL] + keeper
        +------------------------------+
        |                              |
        |       carry, about 30 m      |
        |                              |
   start marker (A), about 30 m from goal
   chaser marker (D), 3 m behind A
```

- A start marker about 30 m from goal for the attacker, and one 3 m behind it for the chaser. Both distances are suggestions; Level 4 moves the start further back.
- Two markers either side of the start line to show where the run begins.

**One rep:**

1. A starts with the ball and goes when ready. The chaser may go the moment A touches the ball.
2. A drives at goal with big touches, looking up at the keeper and the space ahead.
3. Near the box, A settles: one touch to bring the ball close, a look at the keeper and the chaser, then the choice (an early shot, round the keeper, or a lift over a keeper who has come out).
4. The rep ends on a goal, a save or the ball going out. Until Level 5 the chaser pressures from the side and does not tackle.
5. A and D walk back along the side. A becomes the next chaser, D the next attacker.

**With three:** the third player keeps goal and rotates in after each block. **With four:** two pairs take turns, so each pair rests while the other runs.

**The first block:** 4 breakaways each, then 3 minutes easy (passing, walking, a drink); two or three blocks in all. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the runs get slower.

**Load:**

- Each breakaway is a full sprint. The walk back and the other pair's turn come before the next one. Stop when the runs get slower and sloppier, and never use extra runs as a punishment.
- The chaser goes for the ball, from the side, never into the back of the attacker. Pair players by size and maturity, not by age or level alone. The keeper goes down on a soft surface with the hands to the ball, and any knock to the head stops play for that player.
- The keeper faces about 6 runs, then rests or swaps; with one keeper, shorten the blocks to match.
- Level 4 may test a finish after a longer run only once the settled finish holds when fresh; the rest above is kept; stop when the runs get slower and sloppier.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** one settling touch before the finish, so the finish is chosen, not snatched.

**Easier or harder:** easier, no chaser and a keeper who stays on the line. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a game problem with one attacker, one chaser and a keeper. The choice the player makes is the finish, read from the keeper and the chaser.

It does not train beating a defender in front of you or the run off the ball that makes the breakaway. [Slow to Fast](/training/change-of-pace-1v1) trains a carry against a defender in front.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** a 20 m start, no chaser, a keeper who stays on the line. The player finds the rhythm: carry, settle, finish.
- **Level 2:** add the chaser.
- **Level 3:** the keeper chooses each time whether to come out or stay, without saying which. The player reads it and picks the finish.
- **Level 4:** the start moves back to about 40 m, so the finish comes after a longer run. Only once the settled finish holds at Level 3, with the rest kept as in the load line.
- **Level 5:** the chaser may win the ball (from the side, for the ball), the keeper is fully live, and the player has at most two touches inside the box.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The settling touch.** Does the player bring the ball close and look before the finish, or shoot at full sprint?
- **The read.** Does the finish change with what the keeper does, or is it the same every time?
- **The carry.** Big enough touches to cover the ground, close enough near the box to set the finish.

**One question, between reps:** "What was the keeper doing, and what did that leave you?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("one touch, then look"), never which finish to choose.

**Praise** the settling and the choice, whether it went in or not: "You took a touch, saw the keeper come, and went round. That was the right picture, even with the save."

**Leave for later:** the technique of each finish. First the settling touch; the finish sharpens once the player stops snatching.

**When to simplify:** if most reps end with a rushed shot, go back to Level 1 and shorten the start.

## Why we think it helps

A breakaway gives a player time they rarely feel they have, and the chaser behind makes them rush. This drill puts that feeling into practice with a choice at the end, so the player learns that one settling touch and a look are usually there. We judge a rep by the settling and the choice: a well-read finish the keeper saves counts for more than a lucky one that goes in. A missed rep is worth one question about where it went wrong (the carry, the look or the finish). If the player settles well here and still snatches in games, put the same picture into a small-sided game with a long pitch.

## Self-assessment

- "On your best breakaway, did you settle before the finish? What did it give you?"
- "When you missed, was it the carry, the look, or the finish?"
- "How do you calm yourself when you are through on goal and breathing hard?"
