For a coach with three to six players aged thirteen and over, and a keeper if there is one. One player carries the ball to the byline and pulls it back; the finisher holds the run and goes late, to arrive onto the ball at the top of the box and finish.

## Quick start

**You need:** a goal with a keeper (or a mini-goal), 6 cones, several balls, and the width of a penalty area.

**Set it out:**

```
              [GOAL] + keeper
            +--------------+
            |  pull-back   |
            |  zone (top   |
            |  of the box) |
        (A) finisher        (C) carrier, in
        starts deep         the wide channel
            +--------------+
```

- A start cone for the carrier (C) in the wide channel, about 25 m from the byline, and one for the finisher (A) about 22 m out in the middle. The distances are suggestions.
- Every player takes every role: carrier, finisher, and keeper if there is no keeper.

**One rep:**

1. C carries to the byline. A holds the run, watching C.
2. As C reaches the byline and shapes to pull it back, A goes, to arrive onto the ball open to goal.
3. A finishes first time where the ball allows, with whichever foot it comes to, or takes one touch if that is the better finish.
4. The rep ends on a goal, a save or a missed connection. Rotate roles, and run it from both sides.

Count clean arrivals (a run timed to meet the ball open to goal), not only goals.

**The first block:** 6 finishes each, then 2 to 3 minutes easy; two or three blocks. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the runs get slower.

**Load:**

- Each run is a short burst, with the walk back before the next and the rest above after each block; stop when the runs get slower and sloppier, and never use extra runs as a punishment.
- Striking: about 6 strikes a block, then the rest above; full-power strikes in small numbers and only once warm; around a growth spurt, pain near a joint means stop, not push on. These finishes are placed, not struck at full power.
- The keeper goes down on a soft surface with the hands to the ball, and any knock to the head stops play for that player.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** arrive as the ball does, not before.

**Easier or harder:** easier, C pulls it back to the same spot each time. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a technique drill with a choice in it: the timing of the finisher's run, read from the carrier's progress, and the finish off the ball pulled back.

It does not train getting past a defender to reach the byline. [Beat and Cross](/training/beat-and-cross-wide) trains that.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** C pulls it back to the same spot each time. A finds the timing and the first-time finish.
- **Level 2:** C chooses where to pull it back (near the six-yard box, the penalty spot or the top of the box). A reads it and adjusts the run.
- **Level 3:** a passive defender tracks A. A times the run to get away from the marker as the ball arrives.
- **Level 4:** a second finisher runs to a different spot. C chooses whom to find; the two finishers read each other so they do not arrive in the same space.
- **Level 5:** C has to beat a defender to reach the byline, A is marked, and the keeper is fully live.

With three, stay at Levels 1 and 2; Level 5 needs five and a keeper.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The hold and the go.** Does A wait and then go, arriving as the ball does, or drift into the box early and stand there?
- **The body on arrival.** Open to goal and balanced, or square and reaching?
- **The foot.** Does A finish with the foot the ball arrives to, or shape onto one foot and lose the moment?

**One question, between reps:** "When did you start your run, and what told you to go?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet while the ball is moving. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("where was C when you went?"), never when to run.

**Praise** the timing of the run, even when the ball did not arrive: "You arrived open and unmarked. The ball was behind you that time; the run was right."

**Leave for later:** the power of the finish. These are placed finishes; first the timing and the placement.

**When to simplify:** if A arrives early on most reps, go back to Level 1 and start A deeper.

## Why we think it helps

A ball pulled back from the byline is finished by a player who arrives into the space rather than waiting in it, and arriving is a matter of timing more than pace. This drill gives the finisher many runs in a short time, each read from the carrier, so the timing becomes something the player judges, not a rule they follow. Because every player takes every role, each one also learns the pull-back from the carrier's side. Running it from both sides brings the ball to either foot; a player who can finish with either foot has more options when the ball comes across. If a player times well here and not in games, put the same pattern into a small-sided game with wide channels.

## Self-assessment

- "On your best rep, when did you start your run compared with C reaching the byline?"
- "Which arrivals left you unmarked, and what did you do differently on those?"
- "How did finishing first time compare with taking a touch first?"
