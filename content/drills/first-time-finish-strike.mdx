For a coach with two to six players and, ideally, a keeper. One player runs onto a ball played across their path and strikes it first time, with no controlling touch, choosing the corner from the weight of the pass and where the keeper is. Every player takes the finisher's role, the server's role and, without a keeper, the keeper's.

## Quick start

**You need:** a goal with a keeper (or a mini-goal), 4 markers, several balls, and about 20 m of pitch in front of the goal.

**Set it out:**

```
              [GOAL] + keeper
            +--------------+
            |              |
            |   strike     |
            |   spot 12 m  |       (S) server, 12 m wide
            |              |
        (A) start, about 18 m out
            +--------------+
```

- One marker about 18 m out for the finisher's start (A) and one about 12 m out, a little to the side, for where the strike tends to happen.
- One marker on each side, about 12 m wide of the strike spot, for the server (S). The server works from one side for a block, then the other. All distances are suggestions.

**One rep:**

1. A starts the run when ready, on a short diagonal toward the strike spot.
2. S plays the ball across A's path, along the ground, as A comes.
3. A strikes first time at the goal, choosing the corner, with whichever foot the ball comes to.
4. The rep ends on a goal, a save or a miss. A fetches a ball back to S on the way round; roles move on: A to server, S to the back of the finishers' line.

Count clean contacts on target, not only goals: a clean first-time strike the keeper saves counts; a scuffed one that rolls in does not.

**With two:** one finisher and one server, no keeper, a mini-goal or two markers as the goal. **With three to six:** a keeper, and the finishers take turns. **Both sides:** every second block is served from the other side, so the ball comes to the other foot.

**The first block:** 6 strikes each, then 2 to 3 minutes easy (passing, walking, a drink); two or three blocks in all. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the contact gets sloppier.

**Load:**

- Striking: about 6 strikes a block, then the rest above; full-power strikes in small numbers and only once warm; around a growth spurt, pain near a joint means stop, not push on. Most of these finishes are placed, not struck at full power.
- The run is short; walk back between reps. Stop when the contact gets sloppier, and never use extra strikes as a punishment.
- The keeper goes down on a soft surface with the hands to the ball, and any knock to the head stops play for that player.
- With one keeper and five finishers, the keeper faces about 30 strikes a block: swap the keeper every block, or rest the keeper one block in three. A suggestion, as the other numbers.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** meet the ball in stride, and know where the keeper is before it arrives.

**Easier or harder:** easier, S plays the same weight from the same spot each time. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a technique drill with a choice in it: the first-time strike, with the corner chosen from the ball and the keeper. The contact has no controlling touch before it, so the run and the body shape on arrival do the work.

It does not train getting free of a defender or deciding whether to finish at all. [Arrive Late, Finish Clean](/training/cutback-finish-arriving-late) trains the timing of the run onto a ball pulled back, and [Beat and Decide](/training/beat-and-decide-1v1) the choice between the shot and the pass.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** S plays along the ground at a steady weight from a wide angle. A finds the timing and the contact.
- **Level 2:** S changes the weight each rep without saying how. A adjusts the run and the moment of the strike to the speed of the ball.
- **Level 3:** S plays low and driven, sometimes bouncing. A reads the bounce and strikes through it.
- **Level 4:** a block served so the ball comes to the foot A uses less, from both sides. Count clean contacts on target, not goals.
- **Level 5:** S mixes the ball (along the ground, driven, or chipped to the far side) without saying which, and the keeper is fully live. A reads the ball and the keeper and finishes first time with either foot.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The plant foot.** Beside the ball and pointing where the ball goes, or behind it and off balance?
- **The head.** Still and over the ball on contact, or leaning back so the strike rises?
- **The run.** Does A arrive onto the ball in stride, or stop and reach for it?
- **The corner.** Does the choice change with the keeper's position, or is it the same corner every time?
- **The habit touch.** Does A take a controlling touch when the first-time strike was on?

**One question, between reps:** "Where was the keeper when the ball arrived, and which corner did that leave?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet while the ball is moving. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("where's the keeper?"), never which corner to strike.

**Praise** the clean contact and the choice, whatever the result: "Clean contact, and you went away from where the keeper was standing. The save was a good save."

**Leave for later:** the power of the strike. A clean, placed first-time finish comes first; power can be added once the contact holds.

**When to simplify:** if most strikes go over the bar or wide, go back to Level 1 and let S play a slower ball until the contact is clean again.

## Why we think it helps

A first-time finish gives the keeper less time to set, and it leaves no controlling touch to correct a poor arrival, so the run and the contact have to be right together. This drill gives a player many strikes in a short time, each one on a ball that is a little different, so the player learns to judge the ball and the keeper rather than repeat one shot. We judge a rep by the clean contact and the choice of corner, not by the goal. We train finishing with the weaker foot with deliberate practice here, because a player who can finish with either foot has more options when the ball comes across. If a player strikes well here and not in games, put first-time finishing into a small-sided game where crosses and cut-backs come in.

## Self-assessment

- "What did your plant foot do on your cleanest strikes?"
- "Which ball was hardest to strike first time, and what made it hard?"
- "How did the foot you use less do today, and what does it need next?"
