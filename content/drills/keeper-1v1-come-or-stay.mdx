For a coach with a keeper and two to four outfield players. An attacker runs in alone on goal, and the keeper has to choose: come out and smother the ball, or stay big and make the attacker beat them.

## Quick start

**You need:** a goal, several balls, 4 cones, and about 25 m of pitch in front of the goal.

**Set it out:**

```
        [GOAL]
   +--------------+
   |  K starts on |
   |  the line    |
   |              |
   |  about 20 m  |
   (A) the attacker starts here with the ball
```

- A start cone about 20 m out in the middle, and one at an angle on each side for later levels. The distances are suggestions.
- The keeper starts on or near the line.

**One rep:**

1. The attacker goes when ready and runs at goal alone, trying to score.
2. The keeper reads the attacker's touch and body and chooses: come out and smother, or stay, stand big and narrow the angle.
3. The rep ends on a save, a goal, the ball cleared, or the keeper holding it.
4. The attacker walks back along the side; the next one goes once the keeper is set. Vary the start (middle, left, right) so the picture changes.

Count good choices, not only saves: a well-judged stay that the attacker beats with a fine finish is still a good choice.

**The first block:** 6 reps for the keeper, then 2 to 3 minutes easy; two or three blocks, with a second keeper rotating in if there is one. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the keeper's movements get slower.

**Load:**

- The keeper goes down on a soft surface with the hands to the ball, never head first at the feet, and any knock to the head stops play for that player. Once the keeper has the hands on the ball, the attacker stops: no kick at a ball the keeper is holding or smothering. (This safety line is provisional until a keeper technique reference sits beside it.)
- Pair attackers with the keeper by size and maturity, not by age or level alone; contact is for the ball.
- The keeper's efforts are short and hard; the rest above comes between blocks, and extra reps are never a punishment.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** read the touch, then commit to what you chose.

**Easier or harder:** easier, the attacker at a jog and from the middle only. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a game problem for the keeper, with one attacker. The choice it trains is the keeper's: come or stay, read from the attacker's touch and body.

It does not train shot-stopping from distance or dealing with crosses. The attackers get reps of [Take the Keeper](/training/1v1-vs-keeper-finishing) from the other side.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** the attacker runs in at a steady, moderate pace from the middle. The keeper reads the touch without hurry.
- **Level 2:** attackers run at their own pace and play it as they like. The keeper reads it live.
- **Level 3:** starts from the angled cones. Coming out is a different judgement at an angle, and standing big covers a different gap.
- **Level 4:** a second attacker runs in level with the first, about 10 m wide, for a square pass. Coming out may give away the square ball, so the keeper reads whether to commit or hold.
- **Level 5:** a teammate plays the ball through from about 30 m for the attacker to run onto, and the attacker may shoot, go round or pass square.

With two outfield players, stay at Levels 1 to 4.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **The read.** Does the keeper's choice change with the touch, or is it the same every time? A touch that gets away from the attacker or a head that drops tends to leave time to come; a ball kept close or a shot shaping tends to ask the keeper to stay.
- **The commit.** Once chosen, is it done fully and calmly, or half done?
- **Staying big.** On a stay, does the keeper stay tall and patient, or go down early and get lifted over?

**One question, between reps:** "What did the touch tell you, and what did you choose?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet while the ball is moving. When a keeper is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("what's the touch doing?"), never whether to come or stay.

**Praise** the read and the calm, including a good stay that was beaten: "You stayed big and made them finish it. That was the right choice."

**Leave for later:** the shape of the smother. First the choice and the calm to commit to it; the body shape follows.

**When to simplify:** if the keeper comes out on every rep, or never does, go back to Level 1 and slow the attacker down.

## Why we think it helps

A keeper alone against a runner has a second or two to choose, and the choice goes wrong more often from nerves than from not knowing. Here the attackers vary what they do, so the keeper cannot rehearse one answer and has to read each time. A beaten rep is worth one question about what the touch said. If a keeper reads well here and still rushes in games, add the second attacker of Level 4 to the keeper's small-sided games.

## Self-assessment

- "What told you to come, and what told you to stay?"
- "When you got beaten, was it the read or the nerve?"
- "How do you stay calm in a moment that happens so fast?"
