## Quick start

**You need:** two or three players of similar size, six cones or markers, a ball and a watch. With three players, a second ball. Flat ground about 17 m long and 10 m wide: the lane, 3 m clear beyond the gate, and room behind the back line.

**Set it out:**

```
     •        •  3 m  •        •     end line, with the gate in the middle
                 ↑
                3 m
                (D)                  defender
                 ↑
                5 m
                (A) ⚽                attacker, with the ball
                 ↑
                4 m
     •                         •     back line
                (W) ⚽                waiting player, with the second ball

     ←           8 m           →     the lane is 12 m long
```

- **The lane:** 12 m long and 8 m wide, a cone at each corner.
- **The gate:** two cones 3 m apart, in the middle of one end line.
- **The defender** starts 3 m in front of the gate, facing the attacker.
- **The attacker** starts 5 m from the defender, with the ball. That is 4 m inside the back line.
- **A third player** waits just behind the back line of the lane, with the second ball.

**One attempt:**

1. The waiting player says "Go". With two players, the defender says it.
2. The attacker moves towards the defender and watches the defender's body, not the ball.
3. In this first version, the defender helps: at a moment of their own choosing, they make one clear step or lean to one side. From then on they defend for real.
4. The attacker goes the moment that happens, past the other side, and dribbles through the gate.

**An attempt ends** when the attacker dribbles through the gate, the defender wins the ball, the ball leaves the lane, or a slow count of ten is reached. The waiting player counts; with two players, the defender does.

**Who goes next:** with three, everybody moves on one place after each attempt. The attacker becomes the defender, the defender becomes the waiting player, and the waiting player attacks with the second ball. The old attacker gives their ball to the new waiting player on the way, and fetches it first if it left the lane. With two, swap roles after every two attempts. The attacker walks back with the ball, and both take a few breaths before the next "Go".

**The first block:** 5 minutes, then 1 minute rest. Each attempt is a short burst at full speed, and the turns in between are your rest. Stop if something hurts, or if the tackles turn reckless.

**One thing to think about:** watch their weight. Go when it is wrong.

**Easier or harder:** easier is a bigger, slower step from the defender. Harder is a smaller one.

Nobody calls the moment out loud: not the defender, not the waiting player, not a coach. In this first version the defender shows it with their body, on purpose. Later they stop helping.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a game-problem drill for two or three players: a small group, or pairs and threes inside a team session.

**The choice it trains** is when to go, and to which side. The lane, the gate and where both players start are fixed. In the first version the defender's step gives the moment and the side, and the attacker practises seeing it and going at once. From "Make the window" on, the approach, the move and the moment are the attacker's.

It leaves out everything around a 1v1 in a game: a teammate to pass to, a second defender, a goal to shoot at, and what happens after the ball is lost. The last variation below puts a second defender back.

## A full session

- Two blocks of 5 minutes, with 1 minute rest between them, in the first version. Then two more blocks in one of the versions below. About 25 minutes with the changes.
- The defender tackles fairly. Put players of similar size together, and change a pair if one player is clearly overmatched.
- After a failed attempt, the attacker takes a moment to think about what the defender's body was doing, then goes again. Nothing else follows a miss.
- **With more players,** build more lanes. Four lanes side by side fit in about 17 m × 40 m.

## What good looks like

- The attacker's eyes are on the defender's hips and feet during the approach.
- The attacker goes at once when the defender is off balance, and goes fast. Give the defender a second and they will usually have their balance back.
- The attacker goes to the side the defender has moved away from.
- An attacker who is stopped changes something on the next attempt.
- Any move is fine. This drill is about when, not about which move.

## Ways to change it

Move on from the first version when the attacker goes on the defender's step in most attempts. These follow one another.

- **Smaller windows (harder).** The defender's step or lean gets smaller and quicker. Go back to the clear step if the attacker stops seeing it.
- **Make the window (harder).** The defender now defends for real and gives nothing away on purpose. The attacker uses the approach, or a small feint, to move the defender's weight, and then goes. Go back one version if every attempt ends in a tackle.
- **A balanced defender (harder).** The defender tries to stay balanced and to hide every shift. The attacker has to wait for a real window, or make one, and may not find one before the count of ten. That is part of it. Go back one version if the count of ten ends most attempts.
- **Cover arrives (harder, three players).** The waiting player leaves the second ball behind the back line, starts 3 m behind the attacker and becomes a second defender on "Go". The first defender says "Go" and counts. The second defender may not tackle from behind: they have to get level with the attacker or in front. The attacker has to beat the first defender before the second arrives. Afterwards everybody moves on one place as before. Go back to one defender if the second one wins the ball almost every time.
- **In a game (the check).** Play 2v2 or 3v3 with small goals and no extra rules. Somebody watches one thing: in the 1v1 moments, does the attacker go when the defender is off balance?

## For a coach

**Watch for:**

- where the attacker is looking: at the defender's body, or down at the ball;
- what happens when the window opens: the attacker goes, or waits and lets it close;
- when the attacker goes: on a real loss of balance, or at any moment.

**Say nothing during an attempt.** "Go now" from the side makes the decision this drill is for.

**Ask afterwards:**

- "What did the defender's body do just before you went?"
- "You went while they were balanced. When were they not?"
- "You saw it and waited. What held you back?"

**Leave alone for now:** which move the attacker uses. Let them use any move until the timing is there.

**Make it easier, or stop, when:**

- the attacker is confused about what to look for: stop, and let the defender show the step slowly three times;
- the same failure repeats and nothing changes: go back one version;
- the attacker stops trying to go at all: give the defender a bigger step, so that going works again;
- the tackles become reckless, or something hurts: stop.

## Why we think it helps

This is our reasoning, and we have not tested it. In a 1v1, the same move can work or fail depending on when it is made. A lane with one defender gives the attacker that moment again and again, more often than a game does, with nothing else to think about. Nobody calls it, so the attacker has to take the moment from the defender's body.

**Where it should show later:** in a small game, in a 1v1, the attacker goes when the defender is off balance, without anyone telling them.

**What would count against it:** in games the attacker still goes at once every time, whatever the defender is doing. Or never goes, and plays the ball sideways.
