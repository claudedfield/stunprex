For one to four players, with or without a coach. The player runs with the ball down an open channel with big touches and the head up, and chooses a gate at the far end from what is in front of them.

## Quick start

**You need:** 6 to 8 cones (about 20 more for the field of cones), a mini-goal or a target for Level 5, a ball each, and an open strip about 25 m long.

**Set it out:**

```
   START                                    GATES
     +--------------------------------------  [1]
     |  open channel, about 25 m x 8 m      |
     |  push, catch, head up, push          |  [2]
     +--------------------------------------
```

- A channel about 25 m long and 8 m wide, and two gates of 2 cones at the far end. All the sizes are suggestions.
- The player starts at one end with the ball.

**One rep:**

1. The player pushes the ball into space ahead with a bigger touch (laces or the outside of the foot), not a tap, and strides to catch it.
2. Between touches the head comes up to read the field and the gates ahead.
3. Near the far end the player picks a gate and drives through it under control. With a partner, the partner stands in front of one gate and may step across late; the player goes through the open one.
4. Jog back along the side. The next run drives with the other foot.

**The first block:** 6 runs, then 2 minutes easy (passing, a drink); two or three blocks. These numbers are a suggestion: use fewer if the runs get slower.

**Load:**

- Each run is a short burst at full effort, with the jog back before the next and the rest above after each block; stop when the runs get slower and sloppier, and never use extra runs as a punishment. No level is built on being tired.
- For 9 to 12: about 15 minutes, ending while they still want more; the first block's numbers or fewer.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** push, catch, head up, push.

**Easier or harder:** easier, a controlled pace and no gate choice. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a technique drill with a choice in it: the carry at speed, and which gate to drive through, read on the move. It works for a player alone.

It does not train close dribbling past a defender in front. [Slow to Fast](/training/change-of-pace-1v1) trains a carry against a defender, and [The Change of Pace](/training/change-of-pace-carry) the moment of the burst.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** a controlled pace and no gate choice. The player finds the rhythm: push, catch, head up.
- **Level 2:** the gate choice. Alone, the player picks late: the late pick trains the change of line, and the read comes with a partner. With a partner, the partner stands in one gate and the player goes through the other.
- **Level 3:** a higher pace. The touch must stay reachable and the head must still come up.
- **Level 4:** the partner moves late, just before the gates, so the player has to read the picture from further out and adjust the line.
- **Level 5:** a partner chases from 3 m behind, for the ball and from the side, and the channel ends in a pass to a target or a shot at a mini-goal. Pair players by size and maturity, not by age or level alone; contact is for the ball, never into the back.

## In a field of cones

The same carry works in a crowded space. Scatter about 20 markers loosely over a square about 20 m by 20 m (both numbers are suggestions), leaving some lanes open and some tight. The player carries at pace where the lanes are long and with smaller touches where they close, head up, choosing the next lane from what is ahead. Nobody says where the lanes are; the cones show them.

This is a condition on this drill, not a drill of its own: the cones change the space and the route, and the choice is still the player's line. It works for a player alone. The same load lines hold.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **Touch size.** Big enough to cover ground, close enough to reach in a stride or two.
- **The head between touches.** Does it come up to read the field, or stay on the ball?
- **The choice.** Is the carry going to a gate the player picked from what they saw, or just to the end?

**One question, between runs:** "When did you look up, and what did it show you?"

**During a run:** stay quiet while the ball is moving. When a player is stuck, a question or a short cue may help ("bigger touch?"), never which gate.

**Praise** the rhythm and the read: "Big touches, head up, and you saw the gate close and went the other way."

**Leave for later:** top speed. First the push, catch and head up at a controlled pace.

**When to simplify:** if the ball runs away on most runs, slow the pace and shorten the channel.

## Why we think it helps

Running with the ball into open space is a different job from close dribbling: the ball goes further in front so the player can cover ground, and the head has to come up between touches to see where the carry is going. This drill gives a player many carries in a short time, each ending in a choice made from what they saw, so the carry becomes a decision as well as a run. If a player carries well here and still runs with the head down in games, add the chaser of Level 5 or move the carry into a small-sided game with a long pitch.

## Self-assessment

- "When did you get your head up, and what did it let you see?"
- "How big were your best touches compared to close dribbling?"
- "When is it right to carry at speed, and when is it right to keep it tight?"
