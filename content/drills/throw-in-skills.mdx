For a coach with two to six players. One player takes throw-ins from a marked line to teammates in front. The throw has to be legal and reach the teammate; the thrower chooses whether to throw at once to a teammate who is free, or wait for one to get free. Every player throws, receives and, from Level 3, defends.

## Quick start

**You need:** about 8 cones, several balls, and about 15 m by 15 m.

**Set it out:**

```
   receivers' area, about 15 m deep
   +--------------------------------+
   |                                |
   |     (R)            (R)         |
   |              (D) from Level 3  |
   |                                |
   +--o--------------(T)---------o--+   the line (4 cones, about 15 m)
```

- Four cones in a straight line, about 15 m long: this is the touchline. Four more mark the corners of the receivers' area in front of it. The sizes are suggestions.
- The thrower (T) stands at the middle of the line, outside it. One or two receivers (R) start anywhere in the area.

**The legal throw:** both feet on the ground, on or behind the line, at the moment of release; both hands; the ball from behind and over the head. If your league restarts with a kick-in or a dribble-in, follow its rules and keep the read below.

**One rep:**

1. T picks up the ball and looks.
2. The receivers move to get free; nobody calls for it. T reads who is free and when.
3. T throws at once to a free teammate, to feet or into their path, or waits until one gets free.
4. The rep counts when the throw is legal and the receiver keeps the ball with the first touch (and, from Level 3, gets away from the defender). A foul throw, or a throw the defender wins, is a turnover.
5. T becomes a receiver, a receiver becomes T. With five or six, two groups share the line from each end.

Count kept balls, not distance.

**The first block:** about 8 throws each, then 2 minutes easy; two or three blocks. These numbers are a suggestion.

**Load:**

- Throws at a teammate's feet are light work. Long throws at full effort go in small numbers and only once warm; around a growth spurt, pain in the back or the shoulder means stop, not push on.
- From Level 3, the defender plays the ball, never the receiver, and never from behind.
- For 9 to 12: about 15 minutes, ending while they still want more; the first block's numbers or fewer.
- Stop at once on pain, illness, dizziness or a movement that is breaking down; nobody plays through it, and nobody is pushed to.

**One thing to think about:** look before you pick it up.

**Easier or harder:** easier, one receiver and no defender, the receiver standing still. Harder, see the levels below.

## What this drill is, and what it is not

This is a technique drill with a choice in it. The technique is the legal, accurate throw. The choice is the thrower's: throw at once or wait, and to whom, read from who is free; the receivers choose how to get free.

It does not train the long throw into the box as a set play. That is a specialist add-on once the short throw is reliable.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** one receiver, standing, no defender. T throws legally to feet, then into the receiver's path.
- **Level 2:** two receivers who move. T reads who is free and chooses at once or wait.
- **Level 3:** a defender in the area, at half pace, then live. The receivers have to get free of the defender, and the first touch has to survive the press.
- **Level 4:** the receivers find their own ways to get free (short then away, towards then behind) and T reads which one is on; nobody is given a pattern.
- **Level 5:** a small game of two or three against two from the throw-in: the throwing side scores by dribbling over a line of cones about 15 m from the touchline; a throw taken before both defenders are back in the area counts double.

## For a parent or coach

**Watch for:**

- **Legal, every time.** Both feet down, the ball from behind and over the head. A foul throw is a turnover, so reliability matters more than distance.
- **The look.** Does T look up as the ball is picked up, or always pause and let the defenders settle?
- **The receivers.** Do they move to get free, or stand and wait to be found?
- **What happens next.** Is the throw to feet or into the path, and does the receiver keep it?

**One question, between reps:** "Was a throw at once on, and how did you know?"

**During a rep:** stay quiet while the ball is in the hands and in the air. When a player is stuck, a question may help ("who's free?"), never whom to throw to.

**Praise** the choice and the legal throw, whether the ball was kept or not: "You saw the free player before the defender did and threw at once. That was the right read."

**Leave for later:** distance. First the legal, accurate throw that keeps the ball.

**When to simplify:** if most throws are foul or lost, go back to Level 1 with the receiver closer.

## Why we think it helps

The throw-in comes up many times in a match, and it is often taken without a look: the ball goes straight back to the other team, or the moment when a teammate was free has passed. This drill trains the legal throw and the look together, and judges both by whether the ball was kept, so the thrower learns to choose rather than to throw to whoever is nearest. Because nobody calls for the ball, the thrower reads the movement, and the receivers learn that moving is what makes them free. If a player throws well here and not in games, take Level 5 into the start of a small-sided game with throw-ins.

## Self-assessment

- "When was a throw at once on, and did you take it?"
- "How often did your throw-in keep the ball, and what made the difference?"
- "As a receiver, what did you do to get free?"
